# Lucid boards

A Lucid page is a poster. A person stands ten feet away, or holds an A4 Xerox, or sits in the back row of a TAPMI classroom. If they cannot read the claim, you do not have a design. You have noise. Noise is not a loud version of good. It is a failed board.

This skill exists because a 02 SEP 2026 session shipped Lucid pages that looked busy and could not be read: overlapping cream boxes, type shrunk to fit, every hue at the same lightness, website and slide rules pasted onto a wall. Do not repeat that without this file open.

**Trigger evidence.** Defective TAPMI boards, 02 SEP 2026. Ravi: Lucid is not website UX and not PPT UX. The quality bar on any platform is "is this the world's best design of this concept, on this platform." Then: highest text readability. Unreadable noise is useless.

---

## The one idea

**Readability is the design.** Color, grid, and clever composition are allowed only after a stranger can read the claim, the two labels, and the footer without squinting. A page that has not scored 10 from every judge-panel seat does not exist.

World-best on Lucid means a Müller-Brockmann poster that also survives a black-and-white photocopier. It does not mean a Linear dashboard, a dark Gamma deck, or an Excalidraw pastel card grid.

## What world-class Lucid actually looks like

Lucid's own public bar is three Cs: **clarity, consistency, contrast**. Cap a flowchart at about three colors and one or two fonts. Align. Space. Do not overlap lines. That is Lucid Software, "Tips to create wow-worthy Lucidchart diagrams," on lucid.co/blog, and the same three-color cap appears in Lucid's flowchart how-to. It is a flowchart floor, not a classroom poster. Steal the cap. Do not steal the flowchart.

People who design *in* Lucid for a living say the defaults are utilitarian. World-class Lucid is branded information design: a grid, a small palette, complexity simplified, contrast scanned, and color never the only encoding. That is Zoe Heimdal, a Salesforce documentation designer, in the Lucid Community thread "Is anybody doing more designerly diagrams in Lucidchart?" (15 Jan 2026). No public X account for her turned up on 03 SEP 2026. The 13 Jul 2023 community before/after says the same thing in fewer words: three or four colors; too much color overstimulates; curved lines beat a spaghetti swimlane.

A practitioner in that same forum (JdB, Mar 2025) set type at **36 pt** and shapes around **500 x 500** so people could read at a distance. A UML builder at **7 pt** is a known Lucid fail (Greg T101, 3 Jan 2020; Lucid support confirmed the builder defaults to 7 pt). The print rule of thumb that matches TAPMI's back row: about **one inch of letter height per ten feet of viewing distance**. 72 pt is one inch. That is why this skill's claim sits at 56 to 72 pt, and why nothing on the board is smaller than 24 pt.

C4 (Simon Brown) is the grain Lucid users should steal even when they are not drawing software: one zoom level per diagram; if it does not fit one screen or one A4, split; the title names the diagram; a color you cannot explain gets removed. Duarte's Glance Test is the same idea for a slide or a wall: one idea per page; white space isolates meaning. Duarte argues that on LinkedIn and HBR more than on X. Use the test. Do not wait for a Duarte tweet about Lucid.

Color science, not taste. ColorBrewer qualitative palettes collapse in grayscale when lightness is equal. Pair a **light plane plus ink type** with a **dark plane plus white type**. Okabe-Ito (Bang Wong, *Nature Methods*): `#E69F00` `#56B4E9` `#009E73` `#F0E442` `#0072B2` `#D55E00` `#CC79A7` `#000000`. Never white on `#F0E442`. Never white on gold.

The posts below were opened on 03 SEP 2026 through syndication (`api.fxtwitter.com`) and Thread Reader. Primary URLs stay in the skill so a later session can re-open them. Do not invent a handle, a date, or a quote. If a URL fails, keep the URL and mark `[VERIFY]`.

Simon Brown posted the noisy-diagram argument in public. On 15 May 2018 he introduced C4 as a way "to avoid creating diagrams like this," with four spaghetti photos. One zoom level. Title the diagram. Key every color. That is the visual of a failed TAPMI board: mixed zoom, unexplained color, type that only works if you already know the story. https://x.com/simonbrown/status/996312864087597057

Two months later he argued that writing code to make diagrams beats clicking boxes, because a drawing tool cannot stop you mixing zoom levels. https://x.com/simonbrown/status/1011895634654367744 On 4 Jul 2020 he named Lucidchart in that same bucket as Visio, draw.io, and Gliffy: fine tools, the wrong tools for a software-architecture *model*. TAPMI posters are posters, not architecture models. Steal the zoom rule. Do not steal the "don't use Lucid" rule for a classroom wall.

Official Lucid on X is **@LucidSoftware** (South Jordan, 12,711 tweets when the profile was opened). **@lucidchart** is a redirect: "We've moved." The live account sells MCP, Process Capture, and webinars. It does not argue print type size, grayscale, or three-color palettes. Print taste on X is luminance. On 9 Aug 2018, @realscientists: color variation tricks you into thinking a figure is legible; convert to black and white; do not stack similar color *values*; one or two fonts; print at 300 dpi. https://x.com/realscientists/status/1027598341243326464 On 31 May 2022, @iamscicomm: color used as decoration looks like a trip to the circus; warm colors read as foreground; high contrast for colorless environments (colorblind people or a black-and-white printer). https://x.com/iamscicomm/status/1531651972776054785 On 4 Jun 2022 the same account, compiled by @__Matt_Carter__: tape printed type samples to a wall; sans-serif at distance; more text, fewer readers; titles are declarative conclusions; white space increases impact. https://x.com/iamscicomm/status/1532895329045168133 Mike Morrison, 25 Mar 2019: wall-of-text posters fail; one huge takeaway (#betterposter). The TAPMI page claim is that takeaway. https://x.com/mikemorrison/status/1110191245035479041

**Where X and Lucid's "wow" disagree, TAPMI follows X on luminance and Lucid on the color cap.** Lucid's wow post and flowchart guide cap color at about three, then tell you to vary colors, fonts, and shapes for contrast. Salesforce Trailhead's Lucid module and Lucid Community "four tips" (2024) add drop shadows and faded image backgrounds as "wow." @iamscicomm and @realscientists say decoration and equal-lightness palettes die in print. This skill keeps Lucid's three-color cap. It refuses shadows, opacity-as-background, and extra fonts as a contrast trick.

The join: X is where diagram taste is argued in public. Lucid Community is where Lucid users write. Print science is luminance. TAPMI Xerox plus the back row is the constraint. Colorful means **two large planes of different lightness**, not confetti.

Open it when the artifact is a Lucidchart page (or a PNG/PDF exported from one) meant for:

- a classroom wall or projector
- a student handout in color or grayscale
- a Word embed ("Insert Picture")
- a PPT slide that is the board itself, full bleed

Do not open it for ravitejapalanki.com, HTML slide decks, Excalidraw/SVG explainers, or Figma product UI. Those have their own grain and their own skills.

## The grain, said plainly

| Platform | The grain | What "best" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Scroll, 68ch measure, hairlines, dark editorial | Apple x New Yorker. Spec wins. |
| HTML / Gamma / PPT *as a talk* | One viewport, five lines, motion | `rtp-frontend-slides`. Dark for Ravi's own talks unless the room forbids it. |

| **Lucid board** | **A wall. A Xerox. A back row.** | **Two or three planes. Type you can read from ten feet. Lightness carries the split when color dies.** |

A PPT of a Lucid board is not a third design language. It is the poster, full bleed. Do not restyle it into a dark website slide.

Education and grayscale print override Ravi's dark-deck default. A washed TAPMI projector plus a black-and-white handout is the constraint. Light paper, dark ink.

## Readability law

Every rule below is a hard fail. "Mostly readable" does not ship.

### 1. Type first. Boxes second.

Write every word that will appear. Count them. Then size the field to the words. Never write a box and pour the session note into it.

| Role | Max words | Size on a 1920 x 1080 page | Color |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page claim (the only headline) | 12 | 56 to 72 pt, weight 700 or 800 | Ink on paper, or white on a dark plane |
| Plane label ("BROWSE", "PAY") | 3 | 48 to 64 pt, weight 800 | Ink on light plane, white on dark plane |
| Body inside a plane | 40 | 28 to 36 pt, weight 500 or 600 | Same rule as the label |
| Footer so-what | 18 | 28 to 32 pt, weight 600 | High contrast against the footer bar |
| Kicker ("ADS 6601  ·  SESSION 1") | 8 | 18 to 22 pt, weight 600 | Once per page, top left. Not on every card. |

Nothing on a board is smaller than **24 pt**. Lucid's auto-fit will shrink past that. Turn auto-fit off. Set `font_size` in points after import. If the words do not fit at 24 pt, cut words. Do not shrink type.

One line of body is 8 to 12 words. If you need a second comma, you need a second sentence, and maybe a second page.

### 2. Two colors of type. Only two.

- **Ink** `#111318` on any plane lighter than about 50% gray (paper, gold, lime, orange).
- **White** `#FFFFFF` on any plane darker than about 35% gray (ink, navy, teal, crimson).

Never gray-on-cream. Never colored type on a colored plane. Never white on gold (`#F5C400` fails contrast). Never ink on navy.

`#5A5D67` is allowed in Word captions. It is banned on a board.

### 3. The ten-foot test, the Xerox test, the squint test

Stand back. The claim must be the first thing you can read. Then the two plane labels. Then the footer. If a third layer of copy is visible at ten feet, you have too much copy.

Print the page in grayscale, or mentally convert: gold and lime go light, crimson and navy go dark, orange goes mid. If two adjacent planes become the same gray, the argument died. Rebuild with a light plane next to a dark plane.

Squint until edges blur. You should still see two or three big shapes, not a checkerboard.

### 4. Color is a plane, not confetti

"Extremely colorful" means **one or two saturated fields the size of a door**, not twenty chips.

- Maximum **two** saturated families per page, plus ink and paper.
- Those two must differ in lightness enough that a Xerox keeps the split. Pair gold (light, ink type) with crimson or navy (dark, white type). That pairing is the whole trick.
- A thin gold kicker on an ink bar is enough signature. Do not rainbow the header.
- Albers: equal-lightness, different-hue edges vibrate and are unreadable as type. Use that energy only as a border between two empty fields, never under letters.

### 5. One claim per page tab

The Lucid page tab is the claim, shortened. If you cannot name the tab in eight words, split the page.

Four page types. If the story is not one of these, you are inventing a fifth and you will overdraw.

| Type | When | What you draw |
|---|---|---|
| Two planes | A contrast (browse / pay, need / worth, as written / as done) | Split the page. Light left, dark right, or the reverse. No arrows. |
| Ladder | An order that stops you (four questions, four labels) | Full-width bars, stacked. One bar = one step. Number is huge. |
| 2 x 2 | Four named parts, same weight (anatomy) | Four fields, one label each. Not a paragraph each. |
| Table | A braid or a walk (Swiggy, Urban Company) | Three or four columns. Header row ink. Body rows alternate light / dark so grayscale still stripes. Cell type 24 pt minimum. |

Flowcharts are almost always the wrong type on a teaching board. A comparison that has been turned into boxes and arrows is the 02 SEP defect.

### 6. Empty space is a load-bearing wall

If removing a box does not change the claim, remove it. Borders 3 px ink, so the Xerox still draws the box when the fill vanishes. No drop shadows. No glass. No mesh. No icons that repeat the label.

A label and a body that share a fill are one plane. Set `line_width` to 0 on those stacked fields so they do not draw a belt. Body copy **top-anchors** under the label. Vertical middle in a tall field is slide grain. The empty space belongs under the words, not around them.

## Words on the board

The board is not the session note. Steal the line a student can retell. Leave the citations, the tables of pay, and the instructor asides in the `.md`.

`rtp-thinking-writing` owns the sentences. This skill owns whether those sentences are large enough to exist in a room.

Banned on the board: coined lock, classroom clock, "Overview", author-name openings, emoji, section symbol, em dashes in running lines.

## Lucid mechanics (the traps that ate the first boards)

1. **Assisted layout off** for posters. Positions are the design. Assisted layout will stack and shrink.
2. **Standard Import does not set type size.** After create, fetch the page, then `lucid_edit_item` with `auto_font_size: false` and an explicit `font_size` from the table above. If you skip this step, Lucid will auto-fit and the board will fail the ten-foot test while the JSON still looks fine. **Import IDs often cannot be edited.** `lucid_edit_item` on those IDs returns a disposed-Pipe error. Delete the imported shapes. Recreate each field with `lucid_add_block` and set `font_size` at create time. Those IDs edit cleanly.
3. **Do not stack a text rectangle on a fill rectangle of the same size.** One rectangle, fill plus text. Stacking clips.
4. **Table cells** often ignore intended type color. After import, open the PNG. If gold rows have white type, fix it. White on gold is an automatic fail.
5. **Export PNG of every page** and read the PNG, not the spec. The gate runs on what a student would hold.
6. **Canvas 1920 x 1080.** Same ratio as the projector. Print scales down. Do not design at 800 px and hope.
7. Lucid PNG export is the Word and PPT asset. There is no Lucid PDF tool in this MCP. Print from Word or from Lucid's own UI.

## Process (do not skip)

1. **One sentence.** If you need two, you need two pages.
2. **Pick one page type** from the table of four.
3. **Write all copy** in a scratch list. Cut to the word caps. Read it out loud.
4. **Assign planes:** which field is light (ink type), which is dark (white type). Check the Xerox split on paper before you draw.
5. **Build in Lucid**, assisted layout off. One shape per field.
6. **Set type in points.** Auto-fit off.
7. **Export PNG.** Judge the PNG, not the editor.
8. **Run the judge panel** in `references/judge-panel.md`. Five seats. Each scores 1 to 10. The page exists only if every seat is 10. A 9 is a fail. Name the fix. Do not average. Do not settle.
9. **Save** under the session folder (see below) only after all five seats are 10.

If any seat is below 10, do not make page 2. Fix page 1. Volume is forbidden until that page is 10.

## Where files live

TAPMI (Ravi's 02 SEP 2026 ruling): each session folder gets `lucid-file/` at the session root, not inside `supporting/`.

```
S<NN>-.../lucid-file/
  LUCID.md              live edit URL, view URL, document id, page tabs = claims
  document.json         Standard Import, so the board is reconstructable
  png/p01.png ...       one PNG per page. This is the Word asset.
  judge-panel.md        filled five-seat sheet, one file per page or one table
```

`LUCID.md` is the trace. A Lucid URL alone is not a file you can grep.

**Word.** Insert Picture from `png/pNN.png`. Do not recolor, crop into a decorative frame, or shrink until type drops below the print bar. Place at designed size (full content width, or full page for a handout). Lucid MCP exports PNG only. There is no Lucid PDF tool in this MCP. Print from Word or from Lucid's own UI.

Lucid MCP `lucid_export_document_as_PNG` returns an image to chat. It does not write bytes on disk. For a file Word can insert, rasterize `document.json` at the type sizes in this skill (`2_session-notes/_tools/rasterize-print-boards.py` for TAPMI). Judge that PNG. The Lucid editor is the live file, not the Word asset.

PPT, if needed: one slide per PNG, full bleed, no extra chrome. The board is the slide.

## Judge panel (the ship rule)

Never settle. A Lucid page is not shipped, saved as final, or used in Word or PPT until a panel of five named seats rates it. Each seat scores **1 to 10**. The page ships only if **every seat is 10**. A 9 is a fail. There is no average. There is no "close enough." There is no "three tens and two nines."

Score the exported PNG. The editor view does not count. The spec JSON does not count.

| Seat | Who they are | A 10 means | A fail looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Back-row student** | 19 TAPMI MBA-AI&DS students. Ten feet. Back of the room. A4 Xerox. | They can retell the claim after one look. | They can name the colors but not the sentence. |
| **Print producer** | The person who runs the grayscale Xerox. | Adjacent planes still split. Type is 24 pt or larger. Ink on light, or white on dark. Only those two. | Mid-gray type. White on gold. Two dark planes that become one blot. |
| **Lucid native** | A poster designer who has never opened Figma for this job. | One of the four page types. Assisted layout off. One rectangle per field. Lucid grain, not website, not slide, not Excalidraw. | Flowchart of a comparison. Stacked clip. Auto-fit type. Dark cinematic chrome. |
| **Copy chief** | `rtp-thinking-writing`. | Word caps held. The claim is a claim. No coined lock. No classroom clock. No slop. | Session-note dump. "Overview." Author-name opening. A line nobody would say out loud. |
| **Swiss poster** | Müller-Brockmann / Tufte. | Two planes, not confetti. Empty space is load-bearing. Squint test holds. You would pin this at A3. | Twenty chips. A third layer of copy visible at ten feet. Chartjunk. Body copy floated in the middle of a tall field. |

The score sheet is `references/judge-panel.md`. Fill it on paper or in `lucid-file/` next to the PNG. Any seat below 10 writes a one-line fix. Rebuild. Re-export. Re-score. Repeat until all five are 10, or stop and report the failing seat. Do not start the next page.

The old tick list in `references/readability-gate.md` is the Print producer seat's inspection, not a second ship rule.

**Never average a failed seat into a 10.**

## When this skill is the wrong tool

- A **screen diagram inside an article or GitHub README**: `rtp-excalidraw-svg`.
- A **talk deck with sequence and speaker notes**: `rtp-frontend-slides` or Gamma, after the note is vetted.
- A **product UI**: `rtp-ux-design-systems` in Design mode, plus branding for Ravi's own surfaces.
- A **student worksheet**: that is a Word/PDF document, light theme, not a Lucid poster. The board and the worksheet are different artifacts. Do not put the fillable grid on the wall.

## Cold run (02 SEP 2026, what failed)

Case: TAPMI S01 page "browse vs pay."

What a 10 looks like: ink bar, one claim ("The model was fine. The step was wrong."). Left plane Okabe yellow `#F0E442`, label BROWSE, ink type. Right plane Okabe blue `#0072B2`, label PAY, white type. Footer on ink: "Instant Checkout failed the step, not the model." Two planes. No arrows. No anatomy table. No four questions.

What shipped instead: nested cream rectangles, auto-shrunk type, extra kickers, website-palette thinking, a flowchart draft before the poster.

Falsifier for this skill: a student in the back row can retell the claim after one look at the PNG, in grayscale. If they cannot, the skill was not followed.
