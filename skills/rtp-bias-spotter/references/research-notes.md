# Bias Spotter: research context and limits

These notes preserve the original skill's empirical examples while distinguishing reported observations, conceptual models, and local deductions. They are not planning defaults.

## Growth blindspot

The source library holds the June 2026 article, application card, and dossier for **Shlomo Benartzi, Randall Long, and Stefano Puntoni, “Companies Are Using AI for Efficiency. They Should Use It to Grow.”** The [publisher page](https://hbr.org/2026/06/companies-are-using-ai-for-efficiency-they-should-use-it-to-grow) confirms its authors and date; the fuller figures below are preserved from the local source dossier.

The dossier reports a financial-services roundtable belief that an AI-enabled wealth firm could be worth **2.35×** a comparator in three years, a **135% premium**, while participants described efficiency-focused investment. That is an elicited expectation, not a measured valuation outcome or a representative estimate of executives generally.

Its illustrative valuation model has half the cost base addressable and a 10% reduction in that half: a 5% total expense reduction, associated with about a 10% value increase under its assumptions. This does not mean eliminating all addressable costs can increase every firm's value by at most 10%. The dossier also records roughly 50% higher value at 5% rather than 3% organic growth, and 122% at 7%, under a growth-sensitive valuation model. These are modeled comparisons, not guaranteed market responses.

The useful skill question is whether the chosen investment objective excludes a credible alternative. The effect depends on margins, cost structure, growth durability, risk, capacity, and competition. Both efficiency and growth cases need explicit assumptions. A copied benefit may shrink; it does not automatically have zero value.

## Ideation bubbles

The original skill cites **Moran Lazar, Hila Lifshitz, Charles Ayoubi, and Hen Emuna, “Algorithms Trap Us in the Familiar. Can They Also Spark Breakthroughs?” MIT Sloan Management Review, 20 August 2026.** The publisher page could not be retrieved during this editorial check. Treat the figures below as retained local-source reporting, not newly verified primary-study results.

| Novices | 1 cluster | 2 clusters |
| Experts | 2 clusters | 5 clusters |

The table does **not** show equal cluster counts for experts and novices under standard search. A reported lack of statistically significant advantage on another measure is a different statement. The local account records a field sample of 245 and a laboratory sample of 104; the skill's earlier “roughly 60 per cell” arithmetic does not establish the allocation or statistical power. Without the full design, effect estimates, uncertainty, and clustering specification, do not label the null result underpowered as a settled fact.

The NOVEL INSIGHTS synthesis entry dated 26 August distinguishes a shared workspace from a shared retrieval path and proposes varying retrieval before joint critique. Use this as a testable design hypothesis. The ledger's stronger claim that common tools necessarily erase perspective differences goes beyond what this summary can establish. Cluster diversity also needs a usefulness check; more clusters need not mean better ideas.

## Randomized demographic-label test

The earlier skill refers to an unrefereed AI-financial-advice working paper involving approximately 1,000 US adults, but gives no title or stable primary citation. Retain it as a research lead, not verified support, and do not describe its method as conclusively sound without the study.

A randomized-label experiment can test the effect of a specific manipulation while holding other request content fixed. It still needs repetition, appropriate comparisons, uncertainty estimates, and interpretation of the label's meaning. It cannot certify fairness across untested groups, prompts, or contexts. NIST's broader account is a useful framing reference: [Towards a Standard for Identifying and Managing Bias in Artificial Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.1270).

## Reading evidence labels

The previous shorthand combined “audited” and “peer-reviewed” under one symbol. Those are different checks. State the actual status: peer-reviewed study, preprint, company disclosure, audited measure, conceptual calculation, reported belief, or illustrative scenario. Publication status alone does not establish that the study supports the proposed use.
