# Agent Risk: cases and evidence

Editorial review: 13 September 2026. Historical examples are retained with their evidence boundaries. No real agent stop, liability assessment, or incident simulation was performed in this wording pass.

## Expert risk elicitation

[MIT Sloan's 20 July 2026 account](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/these-are-most-urgent-ai-risks-according-to-272-experts) describes 272 international experts evaluating 24 risk domains for 2025–2030 using a Delphi process. It reports 18 domains at a catastrophic-outcome probability of at least 10% under business as usual, versus five under pragmatic mitigation. “Catastrophic” includes more than one million deaths, more than $100 billion loss, or comparable civilizational damage. Information, national security, and finance are highlighted as vulnerable sectors.

These are elicited judgments under scenarios, not observed frequencies, calibrated forecasts, or a controlled mitigation effect. The difference of thirteen threshold-crossing domains is not evidence that thirteen risks disappear. Use the study to inform scenario coverage and coordination questions, not a per-product probability or an automatic budget decision. The [June 3 institutional release](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/press/international-ai-experts-warn-potentially-catastrophic-risks-ai) also describes the study; related publication dates should not be treated as independent replications.

## Human framing and accountability

The authors' [Putting AI on the Org Chart](https://emmawiles.github.io/storage/ai_employee.pdf) reports a survey/experiment with 1,261 managers. The abstract, methods/outcome descriptions, and relevant results were checked; this was not a full 53-page read. The material framing effects concern managers whose organizations already institutionalize AI agents. Average error-catching effects are small. In that subgroup, AI-employee versus AI-tool framing is associated with about 9 percentage points less self-assigned accountability, roughly 7 points lower F1 from a 0.44 baseline (about 16%), and 18% fewer errors caught. Requests for additional review rise about 22 points from 0.50 (44%). These are different measures; do not substitute 16% for 18% or apply subgroup effects to every manager.

The experiment supports examining framing and review design. It does not validate the whole 3M framework, prove long-term loss of judgment, or show that every named agent disables a kill switch. Okposo's “Accountability Must Be Chosen, Not Mandated” remains a separate practitioner source for mindset, meaning, and mechanisms.

## Insurance and employee misuse

[Munich Re's aiSure offering](https://www.munichre.com/en/solutions/for-industry-clients/insure-ai.html) and [FAQ](https://www.munichre.com/en/solutions/for-industry-clients/insure-ai/faq.hsb.html) describe AI performance insurance and tailored GenAI liability coverage. This disproves the blanket “no real insurance market” claim. It does not establish coverage, pricing, exclusions, or legal responsibility for a particular agent; inspect the applicable policy and contract.

[WRITER's 2026 release](https://writer.com/blog/enterprise-ai-adoption-survey-results-press-release/) describes a sponsored survey with Workplace Intelligence of 2,400 employees and C-suite leaders using AI at work. It reports 29% of employees and 44% of Gen Z respondents under its sabotage category, including unauthorized use, information sharing, and refusal to use AI. The total sample is not the employee-only denominator. Do not treat these self-reports as an incidence estimate for intentional cyberattacks, evidence about an individual, or proof that co-creation eliminates misuse.

## Historical cases and unsupported numerical rules

- **Amazon battery pricing, 2016–2017:** the original narrative asserts counterfeit competition, prices of $0.01 within six hours, a 40% category-revenue decline, thousands of actions, a 24-hour audit, and weeks of recovery. It gives no supporting source. Retain the mechanism as an illustrative pricing-cascade scenario; do not repeat this as a verified Amazon incident.
- **Kiro/cloud-billing outage:** the original describes a reported 13-hour regional outage under elevated engineer credentials and a two-person approval gap. That account was not independently verified in this revision. Investigate the actual credential and enforcement chain rather than claiming a separate agent identity is the only possible way to enforce task scope.
- **54-person preprint:** a small human cognitive study cannot establish correlated failures of model fleets. Assess that mechanism using relevant joint-failure tests. Different providers are not inherently independent.
- **Identic AI interview:** the firm/person attachment distinction comes from a promotional HBR IdeaCast interview with Don Tapscott. Treat it as a design category, not empirical proof; portability supplements rather than replaces accountability.
- **Verizon risk dial:** the August 2026 HBR interview describes stated policy in an unfinished turnaround. The 1 versus 8–9 framing can prompt domain-specific rules; its implementation and effect are not demonstrated by the statement.
- **Proportionality arithmetic:** $10 million annual opportunity versus $5 million event loss is a 2:1 ratio of stated amounts, not “50% upside/50% downside” or an expected-value calculation. A 0.1% event rate reaches 500 events only with a 500,000-event denominator. Neither rate nor count establishes that harm is acceptable.
- **Old defaults:** 10×/3× value ratios, four-hour expiry, ±5% changes, 15-minute review, one/five-minute stop tests, quarterly drills, thirty-minute action, initial 100% audits, 10+ reviewer FTEs, and 95% harm capture are illustrative or unsupported prescriptions. Define the actual time, coverage, staffing, and bounds from the workflow and consequences.

## Novel Insights application

The expert-risk and authority entries highlight that answerability, exposure, and control may sit in different organizations. Keep this question, while recognizing that deployers can also restrict use, tools, routing, and access without changing providers. Switching is not their only control. Approval gates can enforce a stop; high approval rates or no historical cancellations do not prove the gate is ceremonial. The risk-dial entry supports clear domain boundaries without proving that a single score always paralyzes an organization.
