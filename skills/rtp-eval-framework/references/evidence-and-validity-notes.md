# Evidence and Validity Notes

Reviewed 13 September 2026. Numerical examples without a retrievable primary result remain illustrative or source-reported, not certified findings.

## Search tools and expertise: corrected publication status and counts

The authors' [Innovation Growth Lab account](https://www.innovationgrowthlab.org/blog/domain-experts-are-essential-for-creativity-in-the-algorithmic-age), December 4, 2025, identifies Lazar, Lifshitz-Assaf, Ayoubi, and Emuna's **Academy of Management Journal 68(5), 2025** paper, DOI **10.5465/amj.2023.1307**. Its experiments involve 104 laboratory participants and 245 field participants. Calling the underlying research “non-refereed” because a later magazine summarized it was incorrect. The publisher page was inaccessible in this pass; the researchers' own account supplies the correction.

That account reports one cluster for novices with standard search, two for experts with standard search, two for novices with the exploratory tool, and five for experts with it. The original skill's “2 versus 2” standard-search comparison confused cluster counts with the reported lack of a significant creativity advantage. The configurations are experimental groups, not the same people all tested under every condition. Do not turn a non-significant difference into proof of equivalence or declare underpower from approximate cell size alone.

The applicable question is how tool configuration affects the measured behavior. Record or experimentally vary it according to the research question. This does not prove every earlier null result is uninterpretable or that all shared retrieval necessarily erases perspective diversity.

## Financial advice: a versioned normative comparison

[Choukhmane, de Silva, Lin, and Akuzawa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01607), arXiv v1 posted August 3, 2026, manuscript July 29, uses prompts elicited from **1,000 US adults**, a life-cycle simulation, and randomized demographic labels to examine advice differences. These are distinct design components; the work is not simply one randomized experiment measuring realized lifetime financial outcomes.

The paper reports LLM portfolio changes moving nearly one-for-one with passive drift, versus an observational coefficient about 0.5 in **Calvet, Campbell, and Sodini, “Fight or Flight? Portfolio Rebalancing by Individual Investors,” QJE 124 (2009), 301–348**. The source skill cited a different AER paper. “Roughly twice the inertia” is this specific comparison, not a worst-human floor, a matched human control, or proof of universal human superiority. Additional context did not eliminate this reported pattern.

The source's 30%→99% “equity-participation accuracy across generations” was not established in this version; do not confuse portfolio equity shares, participation, and accuracy. Keep normative alignment separate from actual welfare and behavior. The example motivates separate diagnostic dimensions, not a mandatory invariant/state-dependent split for all products.

## Supplied-input benchmarks

The preceding library reviews checked MIT FutureTech [arXiv:2604.01363v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01363v3), July 23, 2026: more than 6,000 text tasks and 60,000 evaluations under its supplied-input setup. Keep the task scope and acceptance standard attached. The absence of a matched human-execution arm limits direct substitution claims; it does not mean no human comparison information exists or that a supplied-input score is a mathematically proven upper bound for another task.

Avoid “largest study to date” without a defined comparison and fresh check. Input assembly, workflow integration, customer acceptance, and realized economic value require separate evidence.

## Self-report, rubrics, and falsification

The user-supplied HBR podcast PDF “What Sets Superteams Apart from the Rest,” July 2026, says the researchers surveyed **thousands** of workers and defined about **8%** as superteams using perfect self-ratings on two items: effectiveness in reaching goals and performance relative to other teams. These passages were checked directly. This podcast does not establish the exact “6,000-plus” population asserted later in the source skill; detailed sampling and adjustment methods were not supplied in those passages.

This design warrants care about response style, shared measurement method, and causal claims. It is not, from that description alone, a proven null result or proof that all reported relationships are spurious. Dichotomization discards information in the derived variable; it does not erase retained raw data or make rater generosity unmeasurable by every other method. Comparing maximum-score groups can be descriptively valid. Cutting below the ceiling, retaining the full scale, or adding known items each helps with some questions but does not automatically solve common-method confounding.

The two-site healthcare/legal rubric-and-forum example from HBR's “AI Experiments Need Domain Experts,” August 2026, is a qualitative mechanism account. The four dimensions—accuracy, completeness, readability, patient-centeredness—are useful examples; missing published weights do not invalidate a rubric or require all rubrics to be weighted sums. The sites do not supply an isolated causal estimate of the rubric's effect.

The buildathon/rubric-circularity and decision-grade-analytics/falsifier critiques are the corpus's interpretations. A rubric cannot validate its own claim about an external valuable outcome merely by ranking its favored attribute highly. But an author or AI system can propose a valid falsification condition. What matters is discriminating evidence and appropriate validation, not different authorship as an absolute requirement.

## Agent evaluation and provenance

The primary [Anthropic agent evaluation guide](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), January 9, 2026, supports separating task/trial/grader concepts and capability versus regression purposes. It does not prescribe pass^k as the one universal production standard or require permanently failing challenge cases. The original attribution of judge accuracy rising 42%→95% through prompting was not resolved to a primary result here; retain it as a research lead, not a promised method effect.

Official [SLSA v1.2 provenance](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/provenance) describes verifiable information about an artifact's source and production. The [source track](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/source-requirements) concerns how a revision was created and the process evidence available. This does not certify future maintainability or human judgment. The HBR “Big Tech's Looming Capability Crisis” argument motivates lifecycle testing and accountability; its claim that no golden dataset can ever assess deferred failure was an unjustified universal extension.

A test suite can model modification, integration, load, and selected future scenarios. It cannot enumerate every future circumstance. Provenance complements such checks rather than substituting for them.

## Other retained source leads

- **Coding methodology:** Husain and Shankar apply qualitative coding to AI traces; open/axial/selective coding has an earlier grounded-theory lineage. Do not attribute its invention to AI evaluation practitioners or call it the only proven method for all unstructured data.
- **Conversation matching:** Duhigg's July 2026 IdeaCast appearance supplies the practical/emotional/social lens. Its use as an AI rubric remains a proposed application, not a production-validated defect taxonomy. Mixed and single-turn emotional contexts are possible.
- **Hard cases and differentiation:** the HBR hiring/SaaS and agentic-convergence articles motivate stress cases and distinctiveness questions. Rare cases do not inherently measure reasoning, and common cases do not inherently measure memorization. Assad et al., JPE 2024, DOI 10.1086/726906 is a source lead for algorithmic pricing behavior, not proof that every set of LLM agents will converge strategically.
- **Learning audit:** the source cites Renieris, Kiron, Mills, and Kleppe, MIT SMR, “Beyond Verification,” May 12, 2026. Earlier library research located a different catalog date; do not treat the date as fully reconciled. The reviewer prompt can be useful without claiming a validated effect size.
- **Business translation:** fixed mappings such as context recall −4% → support tickets +12% need actual local evidence or an explicitly modeled assumption. Cost per successful task and the cost of running an evaluation are related but different scopes.

## Numerical defaults and corrected inference

The source's 50/500/10,000 maturity bands, 50–100 trace samples, four/six/ten-hour coding blocks, minimum three traces per category, three-to-five priority categories, 100-case judge set, 20/40/40 splits, 80/70% TPR/TNR bars, 20% disagreement trigger, 85% saturation mark, monthly 20–30% replacement, 60–75% hard-case pass target, and forty adversarial probes are illustrative planning aids. None establishes sufficient evidence for every use case.

Likewise, “eval costs 10–100 times building” and “LLM evaluation costs $0.01–$0.10” are not universal current prices. Calculate the workload with current rates and human effort. A quality score and a business metric can diverge for reasons other than a broken eval. A brief manual review is an evaluation with limited coverage, not inherently a non-evaluation.

## Novel Insights applied with its boundary conditions

The reread of lines 1940–1994 sharpened tool/configuration effects, rubric circularity, and the need to inspect conclusions. It also exposed overextensions: shared workspace is not identical to shared retrieval; a shared tool does not force identical perspectives; a null is not automatically uninterpretable; and an escalation rate is not a handoff count. The revision uses the questions to improve study design without treating those universal claims as established facts.
