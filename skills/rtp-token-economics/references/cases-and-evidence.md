# Token Economics — Cases and Evidence

Editorial review: 13 September 2026. Use these cases to ask better questions, not as current price quotes or causal proof. Historical figures are retained with their limitations so they are not silently recycled into an offer.

## Primary commercial checks

**Intercom Fin.** The [official outcome definitions](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8205718-fin-ai-agent-outcomes), checked on the review date, distinguish resolution, configured Procedure handoff, disqualification, and qualification. The chat/email table lists $0.99 for the first three and $9.99 for qualification, with other contexts separately scoped. A resolution may be confirmed or assumed; a customer leaving after an answer can qualify. The page describes reversal when the customer returns for more help. A configured Procedure handoff can be billable, while certain escalations are not. These are commercial definitions, not independent proof that a problem was solved. The older blanket “$0.99 per resolution, no follow-up within 24 hours” is incomplete and should not be presented as the current rule. Check plan, channel, configuration, and actual terms before reuse.

**Salesforce Agentforce.** The [official pricing page](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/), checked on the review date, offers conversation, Flex Credit, and user-based options. It lists $2 per conversation and $500 per 100,000 Flex Credits; its rate examples use 20 credits for an ordinary action. It also lists employee licensing and a separate Help Agent Resolutions category. Do not describe all Agentforce as $2 only when a full resolution occurs without escalation or negative feedback. A conversation, action, and resolution are distinct billing units. The existence of user-based offers also contradicts a universal rule that agents cannot be priced per seat. Confirm the applicable rate card and additional service costs.

**Cursor's June–July 2025 change.** The [company's clarification](https://cursor.com/blog/june-2025-pricing) dates the initial change June 16, a clarification June 30, and the apology July 4. That is eighteen days from the initial change, not twelve. It describes a $20 monthly frontier-model usage pool in the historical Pro offer, explains the earlier confusing limit language, and offers refunds for unexpected charges. It supports the communication lesson. It does not verify the individual $7,225 invoice cited in the old skill, which remains a reported anecdote, or establish a quantified brand-loss effect. The plan details are historical, not a current offer.

## Enterprise repricing and procurement

The local note for Stacia Garr's August 2026 HBR article, *How to Respond to the Coming AI Cost Shock*, distinguishes Oracle base-versus-premium pricing, a prediction about SAP, and a Workday communication naming January 31, 2027. The Workday statement concerns Application API overages and a grace period. Do not turn it into a blanket deadline for all Workday AI pricing. A current public Workday page confirming that date was not located in this review; verify the buyer's communication or contract.

The SAP split was forward-looking and should stay marked as such. Oracle's scope also needs its underlying offer. The article's dollar examples use the author's illustrative one-cent-per-unit assumption, not an observed vendor rate. Its author disclosed analyst-firm revenue from an HR-technology consortium including Workday; preserve that disclosure when relying on the analysis.

The useful economic question is how a buyer moves between owned capacity and purchased capacity, including wages, contractors, cloud, commitments, and variable usage. This is not an assertion that wages never reprice or that every AI deployment replaces employees. Caps, credit rollover, and grace periods are negotiable design choices, not proven table stakes for all 2027 deals or always worth more than a rate discount.

Novel Insights N/H and the later “devaluable meter” entry add three checks: measure internal implementation and absorption costs; evaluate credible substitutes and exit costs; and track work per metered unit as well as the posted rate. Their causal predictions about supplier power remain hypotheses. A contractual promise that work per unit never changes may be impractical; a reference workload, disclosure, and comparison right can make the issue concrete.

## Renewal, discounting, and bundling evidence

[Miller and Zhang's May 2026 HBR article record](https://store.hbr.org/product/should-your-subscription-business-use-auto-renew/H096OK) describes a 1.4 million-person field experiment at a European newspaper and an acquisition-versus-retention trade-off. The old skill's repurchase bands above 70–80% or below 50%, and its roughly 50% share boundary, are not measured universal cutoffs for AI markets. Actual repurchase under automatic billing is not a clean measure of underlying preference. Test the whole funnel and contribution, with clear customer consent and applicable requirements.

The four discount mechanisms attributed to Rafi Mohammed's May 2026 HBR discussion are the library's reconstruction, not a named formal framework. A discount can serve segmentation without a deliberate nuisance, and a public price cut can be an intentional competitive choice. Assess actual willingness to pay, cost, access, and fairness rather than maximizing friction.

The earlier Broe 44-incumbent study reported 59% bundled, 23% add-on, and 18% standalone. This is a descriptive sample, not a decision rule; the original study was not independently reverified in this pass. The Duolingo April 2025 Cold Call example discusses folding math into its app with language and music. The 2.2 million Apple App Store and 3 million Google Play counts are historical contextual claims, not current counts or proof that a known brand starts with zero distribution. Reused game mechanics illustrate shared fixed development, while P90 delivery cost is a different quantity.

## Numerical guardrails

- Tail-cost thresholds of 5%, 20%, and 50%, Gini 0.4/0.6, and usage ratios 2×/5× are not approval gates. Define the metric, then model the offer.
- A 20% retry rate with at most one equal-cost retry gives expected attempts of 1.2, not 2. With unlimited independent retries until success at 80% each time, expected attempts are 1/0.8 = 1.25. Real retries may differ in cost and success probability.
- A posted $50 output price and $2 model cost give 96% revenue less model cost, not verified gross margin. Include human and other delivery costs.
- Limits on the most expensive 5% of users may protect costs or remove valuable customers. Choose from measured contribution, customer needs, and the offer's commitments; do not throttle a fixed percentile automatically.
