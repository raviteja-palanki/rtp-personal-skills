# Feedback flywheel: evidence and examples

Reviewed 13 September 2026. This reference preserves useful examples while separating evidence, framework, and illustration.

## Feedback delivered to people

[Kluger and DeNisi’s 1996 meta-analysis](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-interventions-on-performance-a-historical/) reports 607 effect sizes and 23,663 observations. Feedback improved performance on average, while more than one third of interventions reduced it. Its theory and moderator analyses address where attention is directed, including toward the self versus the task. It does **not** establish that one third of verdict-style feedback fails while conversation-style feedback avoids that rate. “600+ studies” also mislabels the effect-size count.

Use specific observations, task context, and a useful response path. Ratings, pass/fail alerts, and brief messages can be appropriate; a two-way conversation is not necessary for every signal. Ask what is blocking progress when that helps. Do not claim a single universal threat-response mechanism or transfer human psychology directly to a model.

[Adobe’s March 2014 account](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2014/03/10/lessons-learned-with-check-in) reports approximately 80,000 manager hours saved in its annual review process and a downward voluntary-attrition trend after adopting Check-in. This is company-reported experience, not an independently controlled estimate of the effect of conversation framing. The prior skill’s 30–34% turnover reduction and eight-point survey gain were not independently verified in this pass; do not cite them as established results. The local Ron Friedman/HBR July 2026 discussion remains a secondary lead.

## Repurposing and devoted users

[Sony’s corporate history](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/CorporateInfo/History/sonyhistory-e.html) describes Ibuka seeking stereo music while travelling and a modified recorder becoming the playback-focused Walkman. This contradicts the prior skill’s claim that the Walkman was expected to be a study aid. Sony’s account is a company history, not a test of repurposing as a general innovation strategy.

The local Schonthal and Alt/HBR April 2026 note cites Walkman, Game Boy, Liquid Death, Pokémon, TikTok, and Slack as success stories. Preserve them as research leads until their particular claims are checked. In particular, do not collapse TikTok and predecessor musical.ly into “TikTok was a karaoke app.” Success-selected cases cannot establish how often pursuing an unexpected use succeeds. The worthwhile mechanism is to notice and test unexpected intent.

The local June 2026 HBR discussion of Marcus Buckingham’s *Design Love In* supplies Control, Harmony, Significance, Warmth, and Growth. Use these as interview and design prompts. The material reviewed does not establish a universal sequence or that growth collapses without the first four. Its references to Gallup and Anderson–Mittal suggest investigating nonlinear satisfaction–outcome relationships; they do not establish the same curve for every product or prove a specific 4-versus-5 cutoff. The investor, developer, and patient generalizations lacked citations and are not carried as facts.

## Numerical examples are not operating thresholds

The earlier skill included: 10–20% of feedback reaching a model; 15–20% quarterly improvement; 80% implicit capture; 95%+ gold, 70–85% edited, 30–60% implicit and 50% no-edit reliability; a 70% unattended-use cutoff; 2–3% expert annotation; 20–30% duplicate feedback; 5% and 15% processing cutoffs; and 500 active users as a minimum. None is a validated universal threshold. Set measures using local definitions, evidence, consequence, and capacity.

Likewise, five owner-hours per week, 100–200 gold examples, monthly or weekly releases, and 30–50% or 60–80% feedback utilization are planning illustrations. The earlier Google Maps/Waze “best-in-class” one-to-two-week latency and industry-average two-to-three-month comparison were not verified. A manual loop can be appropriate at scale when the particular update needs careful review.

**Cycle arithmetic:** a two-week cadence offers about 26 cycles per year versus four quarterly cycles: 6.5 times as many opportunities. It does not yield 6.5 times the improvement or prove compounding. Cycle effects vary, may overlap or regress, and incur different costs.

## Novel Insights applied to this revision

The ledger’s K discussion of exception interrogation suggests extracting scoped rules from expert handling of unusual cases, then checking them independently. Its detection caveat means the escalation queue cannot stand for all failures. Needing expert help does not itself prove the system cannot detect uncertainty.

The CBA disclosure discussion suggests looking for one-sided feedback: immediate measurable friction versus delayed or absent harm. Carry this as a measurement hypothesis and test relevant outcomes. Do not turn it into an inevitable under-disclosure law or claim explanations can only affect uptake. Data access, ownership, and accumulated exceptions may matter to advantage, but the ledger’s own reader guide warns that a corpus is not automatically a moat.
