# Safety economics: evidence and numerical limits

Reviewed 13 September 2026. Distinguish empirical outcomes, company statements, conceptual frameworks, and illustrative calculations. Preserve the original archival versions for provenance.

## Privacy and CDR

The full user-provided primary articles were read: **Data Privacy Is a Growth Strategy**, HBR May–June 2026, and Michael Wade and Jochen Wirtz, **Responsible AI Is Becoming a Growth Strategy**, HBR 21 July 2026. The latter offers the CDR matrix and inventory → design oversight → credible external evidence playbook. Its case examples do not isolate a causal premium from AI safety.

The privacy article describes 280 brands over four years, with stronger privacy associated with 12.31% higher **purchase intent**, measured using Osano and YouGov data. Its experiment with 300 US participants and a fictitious tax company reports likelihood-to-use means of 2.86, 4.35, and 5.26 on a seven-point scale. These are neither booked revenue nor willingness-to-pay estimates. The increases are 1.49 and 0.91 points: they do not demonstrate a convex relationship between risk and safety value. The article reports a $869M shareholder-value estimate; do not treat it as audited incremental revenue or a forecast for an AI startup.

The authors’ institution summarizes a separate event study of 360 company announcements across fourteen years. It supports investigating privacy stewardship as more than compliance. The study design and population still limit transfer to enterprise agents. “Visible data dependence reduces a premium” should be checked against the specific underlying model before attaching a numerical elasticity to it. [University of Nebraska author summary](https://business.unl.edu/data-privacy-growth-strategy/); [Moffett, Chisam, Martin and Palmatier, Journal of Marketing, 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222429251367342).

The CDR article’s examples require these distinctions:

- Amazon’s September 2025 Prime settlement was $2.5B: $1B civil penalty and $1.5B refunds. It concerned subscription practices, not demonstrated AI harm or a measured safety premium. [FTC settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/amazoncom-inc-rosca-ftc-v-timeline-item-2025-09-25).
- Apitor’s $500,000 COPPA penalty was suspended based on financial representations. Suspension is not evidence that proactive disclosure bought leniency. [FTC Apitor action](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Apitor-Order.pdf).
- The Apple ATT “96%” claim concerns early US opt-out reporting in 2021, not a universal Apple-disclosed rate. The original primary analytics series was not recovered in this pass. Meta’s estimated loss cannot be treated as Apple’s measured loss.
- The article attributes its 23% consumer trust figure to the Thales Digital Trust Index 2026. That primary survey was not independently checked here. The denominator and question matter.
- Patagonia is a non-AI analogy. Wendy’s and McDonald’s examples do not, on their own, isolate the effects of safety controls. Do not use the article’s Kiro outage account as independently established causation.

Neither article proves that customers never choose safety, or that disclosure always increases demand. A regulator’s favorable response also requires case-specific evidence.

## Standards and architecture

- [HHS business-associate guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html) explicitly includes a third-party AI patient-portal chatbot involving PHI as an example. Scope depends on the relationship and activity; health-related content alone is insufficient.
- [AICPA SOC suite](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2) describes examinations of controls relevant to the Trust Services Criteria. An attestation has a defined scope and is not a universal AI certification.
- [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) is voluntary. Verify separate procurement commitments.
- [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) requires current consolidated-text and applicability checks. The former table’s blanket 2026 deadline and €35M/7% penalty did not identify the applicable provision, entity, offence, or timing.
- [Bai et al., Constitutional AI, December 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073) describes supervised and reinforcement learning using principles and AI feedback. A short product prompt is not the same intervention.

No source establishes generic layer coverage of 60–70%, 80–85%, 90–95%, or 100%; a 2% safe bypass rate; a fixed recovery period by industry; or a 6–12-month regulatory lead worth $10M–$100M. Keep these historical numbers as unvalidated planning examples, never acceptance standards.

## Anthropic case

In its [26 February 2026 statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-department-of-war), Anthropic described existing national-security deployments and said it would retain two contractual exclusions involving mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. It expressed a preference to continue service with those safeguards. This is evidence of the company’s stated boundary and commercial tension, not proof that it simply rejected a $200M contract, earned a subsequent price premium, or excluded competitors from regulated markets. The statement does not establish the eventual outcome of the dispute.

## Novel Insights used here

The Porsche/CBA disclosure comparison near the ledger’s end distinguishes actionability from volume, and immediate conversion effects from delayed outcomes. Its CBA case outcome was withheld in the discussed episode. Treat its AI translation and predicted under-disclosure as hypotheses. Preserve necessary disclosures even when choice or recourse is limited, and measure delayed outcomes rather than assuming them.
