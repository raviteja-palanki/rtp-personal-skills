# Research and facilitation notes

## Best Buy: a listening-first illustration

The supplied skill draws on Hubert Joly's accounts of the Best Buy turnaround, including [his 2021 HBR interview](https://hbr.org/2021/12/former-best-buy-ceo-hubert-joly-empowering-workers-to-create-magic), [the 2024 purpose conversation](https://hbr.org/podcast/2024/02/best-buys-hubert-joly-on-re-defining-your-companys-purpose), and *The Heart of Business*.

Its illustrative scene has the incoming leader spending time in a St. Cloud store, wearing a “CEO in Training” badge, listening to employees and customers. The useful sequence is listening to the work, finding a meaningful customer purpose, and connecting it to management practice. It does not establish that the purpose was entirely discovered rather than also developed, that strategy was not a problem, or that listening caused the financial turnaround by itself. This was a retail transformation with many simultaneous changes, not an AI-adoption experiment.

Use a case like this to make the mechanism concrete. Do not reproduce unsupported chronology or financial claims when a brief illustration is enough. A leader's account is evidence of that account, not an independent causal evaluation.

## Participation, understanding, and respect

The earlier skill proposes three ways dialogue may help: participation can support ownership, credible answers can improve understanding, and respectful treatment can support cooperation. These are plausible mechanisms whose strength depends on real influence, consequences, capacity, fairness, and prior trust. They do not imply that people always defend what they help build or that broadcast cannot change a belief.

The skill previously removed an unsupported percentage ladder for adoption after successive conversations. Keep it removed. There is no verified exchange count, commitment curve, or co-creation threshold here. Measure the actual baseline and useful outcomes, and investigate what changed.

## Career uncertainty and exploration

The supplied material connects Mithu Storoni's discussion of meaningful work, boredom, and self-directed exploration to purpose dialogue. The [May 13, 2026 HBR episode](https://hbr.org/podcast/2026/05/redefining-what-efficiency-means-in-the-age-of-ai) is an **HBR On Leadership** episode drawing on an earlier IdeaCast conversation; it should not be treated as a new independent study. The author's [media page](https://mithustoroni.com/media/) also identifies the On Leadership appearance.

The Google and 3M exploration examples in the supplied skill were named without verified percentages or program-design details. Use them only as prompts to consider supported exploration. They do not prove that an expected deliverable eliminates intrinsic motivation or that career conversations fail whenever roles are changing. Self-directed learning, development paths, realistic expectations, and meaningful assigned work can coexist.

The optional “invitation to play” format came from a practitioner article whose evidence and identity were not fully established in the earlier skill. Preserve it as a facilitation option, with a clear window, useful support, proportionate evidence, and a response path. It is not a tested requirement that every proposal have a working demo before anyone listens.

## Novel Insights: useful scrutiny without forcing visible change

The ledger's L pattern links commitment to negotiating an artifact. Its original two-case claim that dictated artifacts always fail and co-created ones always work is too broad. Later entries propose whether the artifact changed as a practical signal. Treat that as one observation: a cosmetic edit can occur without meaningful influence, and informed deliberation can retain a good plan unchanged.

The Q timing and return-path entries add useful questions: were objections heard before the decision closed, did authority discourage candor, and did participants receive an answer? These do not mean every senior-first discussion fails or every AI-assisted review prevents dissent. Choose the format for the group and inspect its actual effects.

The D cost-and-threat additions widen the purpose conversation beyond a promised benefit. Ask what people lose, what effort shifts to them, and who receives the gains. Do not assume a better explanation solves an unacceptable consequence. These are mechanisms to test and design around, not universal laws about motivation.

The main skill's success condition is honest consideration and accountable follow-through. Increased agreement may result, but the discovery of a sound objection can be just as valuable.
