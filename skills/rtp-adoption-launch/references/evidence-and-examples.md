# Evidence, case mechanisms, and planning examples

Use [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) for the procedure. This reference keeps the library’s case connections available without making every example a universal rollout rule. “Reported,” “survey,” “experiment,” and “case” describe different evidence; none means an effect was independently established in the reader’s organization.

## Adoption curves and headline measures

**Novo Nordisk.** The library’s account describes a 20,000-person deployment, more than 3,000 survey responses, 23% frequent and 74% moderate users initially, 15% of early adopters becoming inactive, and reported weekly time savings moving from 2.29 to 2.14 hours. It also reports satisfaction correlating three times more strongly with perceived work-quality improvement than with time saved. These are case-specific measures with different denominators and constructs. Correlation does not show that a quality message causes three times the adoption, and perceived quality does not establish actual correctness. The [MIT SMR publisher’s description](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/store/how-to-scale-genai-in-the-workplace) verifies the article, authors, July 8, 2025 date, deployment scale, and discussion of a dip, targeted enablement, champions, and governance. The detailed numerical claims were retained from the local account rather than re-audited against the full article in this editorial pass.

**Microsoft.** The original skill and local research note report a 62,000-person sales organization, daily use declining from 22% to 5%, and later reaching 80–90%, attributed to the case researchers’ podcast discussion. Preserve that attribution and the original limitation: the underlying case data were not independently re-audited. The [episode](https://hbr.org/podcast/2026/05/microsofts-path-to-adopting-and-scaling-ai-across-its-sales-organization) summary reports >60% daily and >98% monthly use; the transcript says 80–90% daily. Reconcile definitions and dates before charting.

The champion-pairing and competency-trap ideas come from that local case account. They support trying context-appropriate peer learning and inspecting expert transition costs. They do not establish that the most skeptical veteran is always the best champion, that demonstrations cannot teach, or that an experienced worker must experience the deepest dip. Product, process, and people are useful diagnostic lenses; they interact and are not three statistically independent levers with fixed effects on curve shape.

**IBM correction.** The [May 2026 IBM Think article](https://www.ibm.com/think/news/workers-using-ai-2026-ceo-study) compares 25% regular workforce use, as reported through a CEO study, with 86% of CEOs believing their people are ready. It does not say 85% of employees have access. The 61-percentage-point arithmetic belongs to 86 minus 25, but the figures have different respondents and meanings; this is not a within-employee access-to-use conversion gap. The [IBM primary release](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-05-04-ibm-study-ceos-are-reshaping-c-suite-roles-for-the-ai-era) clarifies the CEO-reporting basis. Keep the distinction between perceived readiness and reported use without treating either as directly observed employee behavior.

**BCG and leader perceptions.** The earlier 76%-versus-31% enthusiasm comparison was attributed to BCG. The linked [June 2026 AI at Work page](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-at-work-why-strategy-matters-more-than-tools) describes a survey of nearly 12,000 employees, managers, and leaders and reports 74% regular frontline use. Its inspected text did not verify the earlier enthusiasm pair. Do not reuse that pair as checked against this page without locating its actual source and question. The lesson is to compare perspectives using like-for-like measures, not to assume every leadership team has the same gap. Reported association with strategic clarity also does not by itself isolate a causal effect.

**McKinsey and value.** The original 2025 figures were 88% reporting regular AI use in at least one function and 39% reporting any enterprise EBIT impact, most of the latter under 5%. The generic [State of AI URL](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) now serves a 2026 report, so it does not independently verify that historical pair. Use a dated source or archive for a historical claim. Enterprise-level profit reporting and local value differ; the gap does not establish that value is absent wherever an enterprise EBIT effect was not reported.

These cases motivate monitoring, support, and honest denominators. They do not establish a universal Surge–Dip–Rebound law or a month-three deadline. Rogers’s diffusion framework should not be cited as if it proved a recurring decline in an individual product’s active use. A productivity J-curve concerns productivity and investment, not necessarily the count of active users.

The pre-launch checks retain these mechanisms without requiring every organization to reproduce the original interventions. In particular, opting into a product is not the strongest form of co-authorship, and a claimed benefit may have a different recipient from the person doing the work.

## Support, leadership, and sustained use

**Practice with workers.** The local manufacturing article recommends mapping tasks and judgment calls separately, learning in the workflow, and measuring handoffs, exception resolution, and effective interventions. These are practical methods. Keep training exposure, measured competence, and production outcomes distinct. A supervisor dashboard needs a clear purpose and proportionate data; observation should inform improvement rather than automatically become individual surveillance.

**Talent decisions.** The Stankey practitioner account proposes explaining a credible rationale, stating objectives and evaluation, and aligning incentives. Awareness and stated support are limited outcomes. Talent systems can reinforce or contradict a substantial transformation, but a rollout does not fail automatically because no hiring, promotion, or pay rule changes.

**Safety and resistance.** Zaki’s argument connects empathy, experimentation, workforce concerns, and support from frontline managers. Treat the causal chain as a set of hypotheses to investigate, not a diagnosis of every non-adopter. Low safety can matter in required use as well as voluntary use. Unauthorized activity can arise from several causes; concern about job loss neither proves malicious intent nor excuses actual harm. Honest communication, fair management, product controls, and incident response serve different needs.

**Audience exposure.** The Franklin Templeton case is a company account carried in the MIT SMR portion of the local library; the earlier skill mislabeled it as HBR. Visibility to clients and partners is one proposed adoption mechanism. The claim that it must produce high use with flat quality is a library inference, not a measured result. Internal peers can also form a relevant audience, and visible work may motivate quality as well as superficial activity.

**Five onboarding conditions.** Buckingham’s control, harmony, significance, warmth, and growth are a framework for designing a considerate experience. Neither a universal sequence nor total collapse of growth when an earlier condition is incomplete was established by the case material. `rtp-feedback-flywheel` can help learn from successful and difficult experiences; top-decile users alone may miss other needs.

**Leadership demonstrations.** The local “Model the Transformation” article distinguishes learning how from feeling permitted to try. Its calendar, uncertainty, and cancellation tests ask whether leadership visibly commits time, admits uncertainty, and makes room. They are practitioner prompts. A costly act is not a necessary condition for learning or credible permission, and passing one test does not establish adoption causally.

**Ownership and local adaptation.** The continuous-change and reasoning-system articles distinguish formal accountability from ongoing responsibility and suggest local modification as a sign of engagement. Treat both as diagnostic questions. A fork can introduce drift or defects; an unchanged program can be well understood and durably owned. Required standardization may be appropriate.

**Training hours.** The [KPMG/UT Austin primary account](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/ai-redefining-career-success-empower-your-talent-thrive.html) describes 523 US-based early-career KPMG professionals with less than eighteen months of tenure, not 523 organizations. Hours delivered are exposure, not a direct test of capability. This measurement distinction does not require treating every claim about sequencing as causal or declaring that hours convey no operational information.

## Agent adoption and data boundaries

The September 2026 agent-trust research note retains enterprise survey figures of 59% reporting agentic-AI use, 9% reporting autonomous workflows, 27% of middle managers seeing ROI, and $202 million intended average investment over twelve months. Keep the populations, questions, time periods, and self-report basis with any reuse. Intended spending is not committed or realized spending; autonomous work is not inherently the right destination for every task.

The same note reports a consumer financial-adviser study of more than 3,000 people, with concerns about privacy (31%), unwanted actions (23%), and understanding actions (11%). These are consumer-study findings, not workplace prevalence estimates. A permission-denial opening scene illustrates a possible problem; it does not explain the enterprise survey gap causally. The published [agent-trust article](https://hbr.org/2026/09/to-adopt-ai-at-scale-employees-need-to-trust-agents) was identified and its public summary checked; the detailed claims above remain attributed to the local note.

The useful design connection is action-level permission, truthful capability descriptions, task-specific limitations, and a real route to monitor, stop, and recover. Avoid asserting that every vendor provides only a generic warning: current product behavior can change. Failure guidance may improve calibration, which can appropriately reduce use in some situations.

Novel Insights also proposes a connection between deeper adoption and provider learning. Its reader guide qualifies that join: exposure, contractual permission to reuse, actual reuse, and competitive harm are separate claims. Check the actual provider and deployment before drawing a conclusion. A private deployment’s adoption curve alone does not test a different provider’s learning channel.

## Later Novel Insights that refine the launch plan

- **Cost and surplus:** compare benefits with the cost borne by the person who must adopt, and specify who receives saved capacity. A refusal of a free tool may reflect this concern, but does not prove it without investigation.
- **Buyer and user:** the local Splunk account adds a second translation when users and buyers value different outcomes. Preserve that possibility without treating one growth-stage anecdote as a universal revenue threshold.
- **Authorship:** the School of Rock account describes different reactions to the same non-AI teaching library and the value of involving experienced instructors in its content. It is a reported case, not proof that co-authorship always resolves expert resistance or that prior expertise inevitably creates a threat.
- **Participation and operating ownership:** withdrawal and unchanged artifacts can reveal questions missed by formal status reporting. Neither is a sufficient test of failure or success.

Relevant reader-guide qualifications and the later entries on these mechanisms should travel with the original pattern D claims. Multiple stories from a single interview or research program do not provide multiple independent replications.

## Replace fixed promises with local decisions

The earlier skill used weeks one-to-four for Surge, months two-to-four for Dip, and month five onward for Rebound; a four-week intervention window; a top-three-fixes rule; a two-week inactivity trigger; weekly communication for three months; and six months of guaranteed program support. Preserve them as possible planning examples only. Choose timing from task frequency, consequence, staffing, and evidence. Measure from the start and distinguish early results from durable effects.

Similarly, persona percentages, mandatory per-team champion pairs, prescribed waiting periods for skeptics, a full-time adoption lead, and a required fork are not universal launch gates. Define responsibilities and capacity for the particular scope. A help desk may be sufficient in one setting and inadequate in another; peer advice may or may not be the best support.

The original trade-off remains: adoption support consumes real time and money, and under-supported deployment can waste both. Specify the local hypothesis, alternatives, costs, reversibility, review period, and evidence that would change the decision. A successful simple rollout with little ongoing support narrows a claim about where intensive support is needed; it does not invalidate all support planning.
