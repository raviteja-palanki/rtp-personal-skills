# Agent specification: evidence and interpretation

Reviewed 13 September 2026. The workflow is a design synthesis, not a validated scoring instrument.

## Decision rights

Greer, Jordan, and Sytch, “What Companies Get Wrong About Decision Rights,” HBR July–August 2026. The user-provided article was read across its substantive decision-role and four-practice sections; the [authors' institution also summarizes the article](https://michiganross.umich.edu/programs/executive-education/insights/what-companies-get-wrong-about-decision-rights).

The article draws on research and advisory work with more than 100 companies. Its four mistakes concern goals, participation in defining rights, role behavior, and rigid hierarchy. It explicitly allows iteration between goals and roles and recognizes some decisions properly remain with senior leadership. The MedTech meeting's illustrative schedule is five minutes to define the call, twenty to debate, and five to decide; it is not a general agent latency or meeting requirement.

Applying these practices to tool-using agents is this library's interpretation. Neither a signature, an artifact edit, nor visible disagreement is a necessary or sufficient measure of commitment. Look at actual decision behavior and resources appropriate to the role.

## Accountability and framing

The [authors' AI-employee study](https://emmawiles.github.io/storage/ai_employee.pdf) includes 1,261 managers in a randomized framing experiment. Average effects are limited; the meaningful effects are concentrated among managers whose organizations already institutionalize AI agents on organizational charts. In that subgroup, employee framing shifts about nine percentage points of attributed accountability away from the manager, accompanies approximately 18% fewer errors caught and 16% poorer review performance, and increases additional-review requests by 22 percentage points, about 44% relative. These are different outcomes and denominators.

This supports checking how framing and operating context affect oversight. It does not prove that all colleague language harms review, that every owner lacks motivation, or that catching errors is the only valuable behavior. The mindset/meaning/mechanisms lens is an organizational design aid, not an experimentally validated three-item accountability test.

## Tool contracts

The official [MCP tools specification, 2026-07-28](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/server/tools), distinguishes tool schemas and invocation/results from broader operating requirements. The main skill deliberately uses a plain-language contract, not a pseudo-schema labeled as a protocol payload. Check the deployed protocol and actual implementation when building one. Tool-provided metadata or annotations do not independently establish trust or permission.

## Novel Insights cross-check

Revisited the decision-rights and negotiated-commitment passages, including the later counterexample about a signed charter that released no capacity. The useful connection is to verify roles through behavior. Avoid turning it into a universal demand to edit an artifact, display costly commitment, or reject legitimate existing authority.
