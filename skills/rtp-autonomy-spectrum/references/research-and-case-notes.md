## The seven labels and cost bands

The seven-level spectrum is a local communication framework. Other taxonomies assess dimensions such as actions, environment, orchestration, human involvement, and observability; see the primary [code-inspection autonomy framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15212). Similar labels across authors do not validate one universal ladder. The source's claimed lineage from SAE driving automation and a January 2026 CSA model needs exact version and scope verification before historical attribution.

Preserved historical cost bands: L1 `<$0.01`; L2 `$0.01–0.03`; L3 `$0.01–0.05`; L4 `$0.01–0.10`; L5 `$0.10–0.50`; L6 `$0.50–$50+`; L7 `$1–thousands` per interaction. The source labels these practitioner estimates but does not define comparable workloads, dates, providers, or full costs. They cannot support a 10× or 10–100× boundary rule. Price the actual workflow through `token-economics`.

Gmail, ChatGPT, Perplexity, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, and Devin illustrate product experiences, not fixed current levels. Tab, chat, research, coding, and issue-to-PR modes can have different contracts. Verify the selected mode and deployment before assigning a label.

## What observed duration establishes

Anthropic's primary [Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy), dated **18 February 2026**, reports that the **99.9th percentile turn duration** grew from under 25 minutes to over 45 minutes between October 2025 and January 2026. It is not average unattended runtime: the median was about 45 seconds. Duration mixes task ambition, speed, user behavior, and product design; longer is not automatically better or safer. The paper also reports greater auto-approval alongside more user interruption with experience. It does not establish that oversight disappears when per-action approval declines.

The source's claims about Fable 5, a Stripe approximately 50-million-line migration in a day versus two human months, Claude 4.8, and most production deployments sitting at L1–2 lack verified primary context in this pass. Keep them as research leads, not current capability, market-prevalence, or release facts. “Adaptive governance is the industry default” is likewise not established here.

## Automation cliffs

Gu, Li, and Zhu's working paper models human-only, AI-only, and AI-assisted decisions with limited human attention and endogenous review effort. The [HBS AI Institute's author-affiliated summary](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/out-of-the-loop/) identifies the March 14, 2026 paper and its later title, “Should Humans Be In the Loop? Human-AI Collaboration Paradox and Automation Cliffs.” The directly linked SSRN page could not be retrieved in this pass.

Treat threshold shifts as a result under model assumptions, with empirical application still to establish. A smooth real-world change would bound transfer to that setting; it would not refute a mathematical result under different assumptions. The result does not prohibit a staged deployment, nor map its routing thresholds directly onto RTP's seven labels.

## Setup verification and scope expansion

**Beyond Verification:** the corpus cites Renieris, Kiron, Mills, and Kleppe, MIT Sloan Management Review, with May 12, 2026 as its recorded date. The publisher-distributor catalog lists April 1 for its nine-page product. These may be different publication records; do not silently choose one as a verified correction. The panel discussion of oversight is not a theorem that checking setup can replace checking outputs. A shopping-checkout change also needs a verified cause: product redesign alone does not prove failure of setup verification.

**Knowledge-work scope:** the user-provided skill cites HBR, “Research: How AI Agents Broaden the Scope of Knowledge Work” (July 2026), reporting roughly 40–48 times machine work per session and modeled reductions of 87% in time and 94% in cost against an assistant-plus-human counterfactual. Keep the modeled baseline explicit; those are not measured savings against a concurrent running workflow. The reported pause-versus-stop pattern suggests a different interaction style but does not prove that every user was only answering rather than steering. More ambitious task attempts may reflect selection and other product differences. Track scope, completion, and value rather than assuming every upgrade creates new demand.

## Governance and human-practice cases

**Procurement:** Himmelreich, Oshri, Scala, and Zaidani, HBR, August 2026, offer graduated-autonomy guidance from interviews. The case motivates setting approval rights, audit, escalation, and override early. It does not establish that controls usually eliminate all benefits, that procurement has one optimal progression, or that governance added late is the most common deployment failure. Contract validation and negotiation can each contain low- and high-consequence subdecisions.

**Review self-test:** “Our Favorite Management Tips on Decision-Making” (HBR, June 2026) is a source for a diagnostic prompt, not a validated autonomy instrument. A list of ten tips is not a study sample of ten participants. Ratification with real rejection authority can be meaningful review; acceptance/edit rates alone do not settle the question.

**Reminder-window experiment:** the corpus reports a pharmaceutical team's change from seven to fourteen days and roughly 65 frontline survey respondents. This single company case does not establish causal improvement or generalize to changing clinical or safety limits. A system following a new numerical boundary may be displaying rule-following rather than independent judgment.

**Permission grain:** McKinlay, Puntoni, and Saka's HBR article of September 9, 2026 includes Workato practitioner testimony distinguishing drafting from sending and recommending from executing. The action-level framing is the library's synthesis. ServiceNow's control-tower example suggests an operating surface for policy and monitoring; the anecdote does not verify every product permission or guarantee adoption. Verb-level separation must still include resource and context scope.

**Four personal-work modes:** David Lancefield's HBR article, August 2026, offers a practical taxonomy and an unnamed COO's four delegation questions. They are advice and anecdote, not comparative findings. The questions include task clarity, quality recognition, error handling, and public representation; they are **not all error-cost questions**. The added reversibility and accountability checks clarify missing dimensions. Continuous synthesis can encourage over-reliance or distance from original signals, but either effect needs assessment.

**Practice-dependent capability:** Dan Wang's IdeaCast framing and the School of Rock Cold Call case (June 2026) motivate preserving meaningful doing and teaching alongside standardized inputs. School of Rock's licensed library and local teaching illustrate that combination without testing an AI intervention. They do not prove that all hands-on capability requires automated decisions, that tacit skill has no observable output, or that every worker must be observed for private learning to occur. Observation matters especially for transferring know-how to others.

**Dissent:** the corpus's Franklin Templeton analyst example describes fact-checking and contrary views. It motivates stating decision rights and record requirements. An advisory copilot can disagree with a human; dissent alone is not autonomy or veto authority. The source's HBR attribution should be checked against the MIT SMR AI-in-Action company series before publication. No legal conclusion about a disagreement log follows from the anecdote.

## Novel Insights applied to this revision

The ledger's practice-dependent learning, accountability/alterability, and domain-specific risk-appetite passages sharpen the action contract. Preserve sufficient learning practice, connect responsibility to usable decision authority, and state which risks a person may take in which domain. Their historical wording sometimes presents these as exclusive mechanisms or universal outcomes. Use them as testable lenses, not instructions to override task evidence.
