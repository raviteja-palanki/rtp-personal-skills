# Vision Setting — Examples and Evidence

Editorial review: 13 September 2026. All unnamed product and enterprise cases in this skill are illustrations. They are not verified deployments, customer quotes, competitor assessments, or performance commitments.

## The industrial example, made reviewable

The earlier case was labeled a real Fortune 100 enterprise example from mid-2024 but did not identify a company or supply evidence. Its starting statement was “AI-powered predictive maintenance for industrial customers.” That wording is broad and leaves the intended experience unclear. It can still be a useful product-category description; it does not prove nobody believes the vision or that every five-quality test fails.

The proposed focus is an experienced shift lead. The old examples used operators with 10–30 years of experience and a specific 25-year judgment story across refineries, chemical plants, and aerospace assembly. Those settings may have different jobs, assets, risks, and constraints. Select a primary use context before assuming one experience serves them all.

The fictional narrative included compressor drift over eleven days, vibration, oil temperature, and current signals, and a repair scheduled two days later. These are plausible story elements to validate, not recorded sensor findings or a claim that the system can predict every failure. A useful demonstration shows evidence, uncertainty, confirmation steps, and the operator's authority.

The old headline promised that every machine about to fail would explain why. It exceeded its stated fifteen-word target and made an absolute performance claim. The revised headline describes evidence-supported maintenance decisions without promising perfect detection. Ambition can remain high while the limits are stated clearly.

### What the illustration could prioritize

- Evidence and uncertainty that help the operator assess a warning.
- Usability in the actual environment, including device, connectivity, language, accessibility, and workload.
- Connection to maintenance planning, reliable data, and recovery from a wrong or missing alert.

These are hypotheses for discovery. A generated failure narrative may help or mislead; explanations must be evaluated. A tablet is not always the right interface. Executive reporting or other supporting users may be necessary to fund, schedule, or govern the operator's work.

The old exclusions—every executive dashboard, every black-box model, and all anomaly detection without a narrative—were too broad. The customer benefit and operating requirements determine the boundary. No evidence supports the assertion that these exclusions removed 30% of an actual roadmap.

### Claims to verify before reuse

The $50 billion annual US downtime estimate, a 50% downtime reduction within eighteen months, the largest installed base, and a unique end-to-end ability were not supported in the original case. Neither were the characterizations of GE and Siemens as unable to express operator-centered value. Do not present them as facts or use them to award a 5/5 differentiation score.

Statements attributed to fictional operators, including preferring the system to their own intuition, are illustrative reactions. Greater trust is not automatically the desired outcome; appropriate reliance on demonstrated performance is. An older operator choosing to work beyond retirement is one possible personal preference, not a universal motivation or product-success criterion.

## Preserve the other examples without overpromising

| Original illustration | Clearer use |
|---|---|
| Knowledge workers think clearly under pressure | A broad direction; identify the task and evidence of improvement before treating it as a specific product vision. |
| Solo lawyers handle a four-person workload without burnout | A hypothetical aspiration requiring evidence about workload, quality, professional responsibilities, and well-being. |
| Hospital pharmacists catch every interaction risk | An ambition to reduce missed risks, not a guarantee of perfect clinical detection. Release criteria need their own evaluation and authorized review. |
| Field engineers diagnose any unfamiliar machine within five minutes using sight and sound | A deliberately ambitious scenario; equipment scope, hazards, available information, and performance claims need validation. |
| Associates working sixty-plus hours lose three nights weekly to review, then finish in ninety minutes | Fictional before/after. Do not claim measured time savings, improved retention, or that all released time reaches the family. |
| Lawyers understand 200 hours of weekend depositions by Monday | An aspirational workflow example; completeness and verified understanding are distinct from generated summaries. |
| Primary-care physicians reduce charting by one hour daily and spend it with patients | A proposed benefit pathway, not a measured effect. Time saved may have other appropriate destinations. |
| A developer ships in thirty minutes what once took three days | A technical-outcome illustration; use matched tasks, quality, dependencies, and elapsed versus active-time measures. |

A large context window does not by itself make document summarization obsolete. The original reference to one-million-token windows is a capability example that needs model/date verification, not proof that a user job disappeared. Future model names in the old case were hypothetical; the main skill uses capability scenarios instead.

## Horizons, cuts, and quality checks

One-, three-, and ten-year horizons are planning choices. Model generations, cost changes, and regulation do not arrive at fixed counts per horizon. A vision older than eighteen months can remain useful, while a recent one can need immediate revision.

The three drafts, fifteen-word headline, three progress signals, three priorities and exclusions, thirty-percent roadmap cut, and sixty-percent alignment target were instructional heuristics. The additional seventy-five-percent mature-team and forty-to-fifty-percent discovery-team thresholds had no stated calibration evidence. Keep their intended questions—clarity, focus, coherence, and allocation—without requiring the numbers.

The five-quality frame is this library's synthesis. [SVPG's Vision vs. Strategy](https://www.svpg.com/vision-vs-strategy/) discusses vision, strategy, and several ways to convey the intended future, including narrative, storyboard, and visiontype. [The Power of Visiontypes](https://www.svpg.com/the-power-of-visiontypes/) describes prototypes as a way to communicate vision. These sources do not validate the library's five-pass scoring rule or roadmap-cut quota.

## Efficiency, growth, and capacity

The earlier synthesis drew on HBR's June 2026 *Companies Are Using AI for Efficiency. They Should Use It to Grow*, May 2026 *Redefining What Efficiency Means in the Age of AI*, and a cognitive-state discussion. Treat the resulting framework as an argument to test, not a universal measured law.

For example, revenue of 100 and cost of 90 gives profit of 10. A 5% reduction in cost raises profit to 14.5: a 45% profit increase. That is not a claim about firm valuation, but it shows why a roughly 10% ceiling on the value of efficiency cannot be generalized. Growth has costs, risks, and limits too.

Threefold proposals, fourfold account coverage, and a 60% cycle-time reduction can be useful measures when connected to customer outcomes, quality, costs, and relevant constraints. Throughput does not become worthless merely because AI contributes to it. If released capacity is part of the value case, name its destination and prerequisites, then measure whether the intended benefit occurs.

Novel Insights D/L/Q motivate checking benefits in the recipient's terms and involving people who must act on the vision. Their stronger claims—that a shared artifact must change, disagreement must occur, or co-creation always produces commitment—are not requirements. A roadmap that already fits can be confirmed. A change may be needed in resources or authority rather than in the words.
