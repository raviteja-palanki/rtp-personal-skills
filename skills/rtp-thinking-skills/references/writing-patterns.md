# Writing patterns to review

This reference preserves the twenty-four distinctions from the earlier thinking skill. Use `rtp-thinking-writing` as the primary standard. Consult this list when a passage sounds inflated, repetitive, or difficult to follow; it is not a requirement to rewrite source quotations or to remove every listed word regardless of meaning.

The earlier file attributed the taxonomy to Wikipedia's WikiProject AI Cleanup. That is retained as provenance for the pattern list, not evidence that a pattern reliably identifies AI authorship.

## Content

1. **Inflated significance:** replace unsupported phrases such as "pivotal moment" or "enduring testament" with the specific event and its consequence.
2. **Borrowed notability:** identify the relevant source and why it matters instead of using a list of prestigious names as evidence.
3. **Apparent analysis without a mechanism:** check whether phrases such as "highlighting," "showcasing," or "reflecting" explain a relationship or merely suggest one.
4. **Promotion:** replace unsupported descriptions such as "breathtaking" or "groundbreaking" with concrete attributes.
5. **Vague attribution:** name the source behind "experts believe," qualify the claim, or remove it.
6. **Generic challenge narratives:** describe the actual challenge, response, and result instead of a stock "despite challenges, it continues to thrive" passage.

## Language

7. **Unnecessary elevated vocabulary:** prefer a familiar term when words such as "delve," "foster," "underscore," "intricate," or an abstract "landscape" add no precision.
8. **Indirect descriptions:** use "is" or "has" where "serves as," "stands as," or "boasts" adds nothing.
9. **Formulaic contrasts:** replace repeated "not just X, but Y" constructions with a direct explanation of the relationship.
10. **Forced groups:** let the content determine the number of items instead of always grouping ideas in threes.
11. **Synonym cycling:** use one clear term consistently when several alternatives name the same thing.
12. **False ranges:** check that a "from X to Y" phrase describes a meaningful range rather than two unrelated examples.

## Presentation

13. **Excessive em dashes:** use normal sentence structure in running prose, following the standing writing preference.
14. **Excessive bold:** emphasize only what helps the reader navigate or interpret the text.
15. **Unnecessary inline-header lists:** use connected prose where it better explains the thought; retain lists for parallel items.
16. **Headings:** use clear sentence-case headings unless an established format requires otherwise.
17. **Decorative emoji:** omit them in professional prose; preserve meaningful source notation or an explicitly requested use.
18. **Quotes:** use straight quotes where appropriate while preserving exact quotations, source titles, and functional syntax.

## Communication

19. **Stock assistant language:** start with the substance instead of automatic phrases such as "Great question!" or "Certainly!"
20. **Unhelpful disclaimers:** explain a specific access or evidence limitation when it matters, rather than inserting a generic knowledge-cutoff statement. Do not conceal a real limitation.
21. **Agreement without judgment:** address the substance and offer respectful disagreement when the evidence warrants it.

## Filler and uncertainty

22. **Wordy connectors:** prefer "to" over "in order to" and "because" over "due to the fact that" where the meaning is unchanged.
23. **Stacked hedging:** replace "could potentially possibly" with a single accurate qualification and explain the uncertainty where needed.
24. **Generic endings:** finish with the relevant consequence, fact, or next action rather than an unsupported prediction that "the future looks bright."

Natural voice comes from a real point, concrete language, appropriate uncertainty, and sentence rhythm that follows the thought. State a position when the task calls for one. Recognize complexity without inventing personal feelings or adding deliberate disorder to appear human.
