# Interview synthesis: method and evidence boundaries

Reviewed 13 September 2026.

## What the three passes mean here

This skill uses descriptive coding, theme development, and decision-focused selection as a practical product-research workflow. Open, axial, and selective coding have particular methodological histories; their use here does not establish that the project meets the requirements of a grounded-theory study. Nor are all AI evaluation teams known to use this exact sequence. Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar remain relevant practitioner leads for evaluation, not evidence that one qualitative method is universally required.

[Braun and Clarke’s methodological FAQ](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/faqs/) distinguishes reflexive thematic analysis from coding-reliability approaches. In their approach, themes are developed through the researcher’s engagement with data; they do not simply appear without interpretation. They do not advocate independent coders and inter-rater reliability as quality requirements for reflexive thematic analysis. This does not prohibit a product team from using a shared codebook or comparing coding; it means the team must state its approach rather than mix incompatible claims about rigor.

## How many interviews are enough?

[Malterud, Siersma, and Guassora](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26613970/) propose information power as a guide, considering the study aim, sample specificity, established theory, dialogue quality, and analysis strategy. Their argument supports judging sample adequacy in context, not declaring all samples below three useless or every set of five to fifteen sufficient.

The earlier skill’s 50–150 codes per half-hour, eight-to-fifteen themes, three-to-five selected themes, and 30% double-coding fraction are planning illustrations. They are not established cutoffs. A count of labels cannot by itself demonstrate depth or reliability.

## Novel Insights applied

The ledger’s reader guide requires separating observation, claim, and inference and carrying later qualifications forward. Its August discussions of framed interviews and self-report caution against treating accounts as direct measurement. The September Spataro discussion also preserves candid remarks within a commercially interested interview: incentives are relevant context, not a reason to discard the entire conversation.

The ledger’s judgment and explanation discussions distinguish concerns about capability from measured capability change and distinguish persuasive explanation from verification. Those distinctions matter in the evaluation bridge: a skill-loss concern needs an appropriate capability study, while an explanation feature needs checks of decision quality as well as acceptance. The ledger’s broad claims about explanations are not universal laws and should be read with the counterevidence recorded in the revised AI UX and Judgment Guard skills.
