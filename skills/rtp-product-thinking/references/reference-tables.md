# Product judgment reference tables

Use these tables as decision aids. Defaults need evidence and context; they are not automatic classifications of people or permission to bypass required controls.

## 1. Reversibility

| Decision | Appropriate approach |
|---|---|
| Difficult or costly to reverse | Seek proportionate evidence, include affected expertise, examine failure and recovery, and clarify who commits. Delay also has a cost. |
| Easy to reverse with bounded consequences | Use a small action or experiment when it is informative; define monitoring and rollback. Verify that the claimed reversibility is real. |

## 2. Feedback

| Source | What to examine | Useful response |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated target-user feedback | Independence, segment, workflow, severity, and proposed remedy | Investigate the need and alternatives; frequency does not prove the requested feature is right |
| One target-user report | Severity and what it reveals | A single critical defect may warrant action; otherwise retain it for investigation |
| Non-target user | Whether the issue affects access, safety, future users, or a mistaken segment assumption | Assess the issue rather than discard by identity |
| Internal stakeholder | Relevant expertise, authority, incentives, and obligations | Verify the substance and clarify the decision needed |
| Competitor behavior | Their context and what is actually observed | Treat copying as a signal, not validation of your strategy |
| Domain expert | Relevant expertise, evidence, and possible limits | Consider seriously and investigate disagreement |
| Data that conflicts with intuition | Measurement quality, population, causal explanation, and the original expectation | Test both the data interpretation and the intuition |

## 3. Meeting purpose

| Purpose | Desired result | Common risk |
|---|---|---|
| Inform | Shared understanding and necessary questions resolved | Unclear relevance or excessive detail |
| Discuss | Useful exploration and a clearer problem or option set | Premature convergence or discussion without a next step |
| Decide | An explicit decision, owner, conditions, and follow-up | Repeated debate without the necessary authority or evidence |
| Build the relationship | Better mutual understanding and trust | Forced participation or assuming time together alone builds trust |

A meeting can serve more than one purpose. State the primary need and adapt the format; a written update may suffice.

## 4. Conflict

| Possible cause | What to do |
|---|---|
| Different discussion levels | Clarify whether the issue is impact, execution, or understanding of the work |
| Different information | Compare sources and uncertainties; identify what remains disputed |
| Different values or priorities | Name the trade-off, negotiate within authority, or escalate the decision |
| Interaction-style mismatch | Describe the behavior and agree working expectations without diagnosing personality |
| Authority or ownership conflict | Clarify decision rights, accountability, and resources rather than assuming a status motive |
| Indirect or inconsistent accounts | Use a shared factual record and an appropriate joint conversation; retain necessary confidentiality |

## 5. AI assistance by task

| Task class | Useful assistance | Required judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead | Drafting routine updates, organizing material, or suggesting schedules | Check accuracy, confidentiality, permissions, and actual side effects |
| Neutral | Accelerating analysis, documentation, or recurring work | Inspect assumptions, missing nuance, and fitness for the task |
| Leverage | Generating alternatives, critiquing strategy, exploring evidence, or supporting a consequential decision | Interrogate the reasoning and evidence; retain accountable choice and appropriate domain controls |

LNO measures the value of attention, not AI risk. A routine scheduling action can disclose information or create a commitment. A high-value strategy discussion can use AI productively. Avoid both unexamined delegation and a blanket ban on AI-supported thinking.

## 6. Product stage and method

| Activity | Explore | Expand | Extract |
|---|---|---|---|
| A/B or other experiments | Use when the question, sample, and exposure conditions fit; prototypes may be better | Test growth and experience hypotheses with relevant guardrails | Test worthwhile changes where experimentation is appropriate; not every decision needs it |
| Economic projections | Use bounded scenarios and assumptions to check plausibility | Refine unit economics and scaling needs | Track realized economics and compare alternatives |
| Documentation | Record decisions, responsibilities, and critical controls proportionately | Support coordination and repeatable delivery | Maintain essential knowledge without unnecessary process |
| Delivery measures | Use to manage learning work, not as proof of customer value | Track delivery constraints alongside outcomes | Monitor reliability and improvement without mistaking velocity for value |
| Customer research | Discover needs and test assumptions | Learn about adoption, segments, and changing needs | Detect new needs and threats as well as optimize existing work |
| Risk-taking | Bound the downside while learning | Balance growth with reliability and capacity | Consider renewal and replacement bets, not only preserving the status quo |

## 7. Stakeholder motivation hypotheses

| Statement | Plausible interpretations | Clarifying response |
|---|---|---|
| “Let's get more data.” | A real evidence gap, unclear criteria, or reluctance to decide | Which observation would change the decision, and how can we obtain it? |
| “This is a priority.” | A consequential need, obligation, or desire to be heard | What outcome and deadline matter, and what should it displace? |
| “We need alignment.” | Missing input, conflicting incentives, unclear authority, or exclusion | Which disagreement remains, and whose decision or commitment is needed? |
| “What's the ROI?” | A funding requirement, sustainability concern, or comparison with alternatives | Explain expected value, cost, uncertainty, and the decision the estimate supports |
| “It's too risky.” | A real hazard, unclear controls, or incomplete understanding | Which failure concerns you, how severe is it, and what evidence or control would help? |
| “Let's discuss offline.” | Confidentiality, limited time, conflict, or missing participants | Agree who should join, what is private, and how any decision will be recorded |

These are possibilities to ask about, not a decoder of hidden intent. Supply accurate substance rather than a soundbite designed to secure compliance.

## Six pairs of apparently conflicting advice

| Advice pair | Context-sensitive resolution |
|---|---|
| Avoid micromanagement / details matter | Set involvement by complexity, risk, skill, and decision needs. Explain scope and exit conditions; neither neglect nor constant control is the default. |
| Follow data / trust intuition | Form expectations and test them with relevant evidence. Both can help in innovation and optimization; neither owns a product stage. |
| Work hard / allocate effort wisely | Match attention, capacity, and recovery to the work. Long hours can be temporarily necessary but are not a career-stage requirement or a substitute for prioritization. |
| Be authentic / make work visible | Communicate truthfully about outcomes, uncertainty, and needs. Visibility can matter; pretending certainty or fixing a universal daily optics quota does not follow. |
| Use frameworks / exercise judgment | Learn the assumptions behind a framework and retain procedures that prevent real errors. Adapt irrelevant steps without discarding controls merely because you feel expert. |
| Take ownership / avoid burnout | Own a proportionate next action, clarify dependencies, and seek the needed authority or capacity. Escalation and reliable delegation support agency. |

## Twelve anti-pattern checks

The original cautions remain as questions, not slogans:

1. Are you overstating competence or certainty to appear confident?
2. Does demanding a solution discourage someone from surfacing an important unsolved problem?
3. Would clearer decisions help more than another process step, or is a real process control missing?
4. Does the proposed best practice fit this context and its evidence?
5. Is a consequential irreversible launch being used as an unnecessarily risky experiment?
6. Is waiting for data informative, or a way to avoid forming a reasoned expectation?
7. Does the decision include relevant expertise and affected perspectives without confusing input with decision authority?
8. Are the skill's steps improving judgment or being performed mechanically?
9. Does “alignment” mean resolving a real disagreement, or postponing a decision?
10. Will the proposed A/B test answer a consequential question under appropriate conditions?
11. Does a customer request reveal an important need, and what alternatives could serve it?
12. Does “data-driven” describe a defensible inference, or obscure the decision-maker's responsibility?
