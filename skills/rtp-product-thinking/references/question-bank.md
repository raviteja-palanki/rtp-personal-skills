# Product thinking: 100 questions

Choose the few questions that clarify the decision. These are prompts for inquiry, not a scorecard, interview script, or evidence that the implied problem exists. The six core groups contain fifteen questions each; the AI group adds ten.

## Product quality

1. Without the logo, what would distinguish this product from alternatives?
2. What coherent point of view connects the features?
3. How does this serve customers, and are internal rewards distorting the choice?
4. What would a demanding critic we respect find strong or weak?
5. What would a simpler version look like?
6. What evidence connects the stated request to an underlying need?
7. If this worked exactly as intended, what valuable outcome would change?
8. Does this reduce users' work or add complexity they must manage?
9. What do we believe about the market that competitors may overlook?
10. As a prioritization exercise, which three capabilities would we protect first, and why?
11. Is the need emerging, already important, or insufficiently established?
12. What would make switching worthwhile, including price, effort, and risk?
13. What job is the customer trying to get done?
14. Can someone unfamiliar with the product understand its value from our explanation?
15. What would customers do if we did not build this?

Question 14 is a **clarity test**. The original called it the “Mom Test”; Rob Fitzpatrick's [Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/) concerns customer conversations that reduce biased feedback, not whether a non-user understands an explanation.

## Decision quality

1. Compared with feasible alternatives, why is this a good use of limited resources?
2. Which consequences are difficult to reverse, and what would reversal require?
3. What are we choosing, delaying, or declining, and what capacity does that release?
4. Are we debating solutions before agreeing on the problem?
5. How would the choice change if impressing others were irrelevant?
6. With half the time, what would we retain, reduce, or renegotiate?
7. With twice the resources, what would actually become possible?
8. Can we preserve options through a smaller commitment or staged decision?
9. What follows if this succeeds, fails, or produces an inconclusive result?
10. Is a recent discussion receiving more weight than its evidence warrants?
11. What information would change the decision?
12. Who owns the decision, and do their authority and responsibilities match?
13. What indirect or delayed effects should we consider?
14. What is the cost of a wrong decision compared with the cost of delay?
15. Are we making a decision or deferring one, and is that deliberate?

## Strategy

1. How might a capable competitor respond, and what does that reveal about our strategy?
2. Which important belief differs from conventional wisdom, and what supports it?
3. What market insight makes this choice promising?
4. Can we explain the strategy in plain language?
5. Which current or future customers are we serving, and what changes between them?
6. What advantage do we have, and how does this choice strengthen or use it?
7. Does the plan explain a theory of success, or only list activities?
8. What must be true for the strategy to work?
9. What will we stop, defer, or fund differently to make room?
10. If execution is strong but the outcome disappoints, which assumptions could we test?
11. Is the advantage durable or temporary, and is either sufficient for this objective?
12. Where are we improving existing performance, differentiating, or doing both?
13. Why should this approach succeed for these customers in this market?
14. How resilient is it to plausible competitor responses?
15. Would a neutral reader find our public explanation coherent with our actions?

## Customer understanding

1. How important is this need relative to other priorities, and does its severity warrant attention regardless of rank?
2. How do customers address it today?
3. What existing behavior would they change or stop to adopt the product?
4. What have we learned that challenged our expectations?
5. What reaction do we predict to a new design, and how will we test that prediction?
6. Do we understand practical constraints as well as preferences?
7. Where do reported preferences and observed behavior agree or differ, and why might that be?
8. Are we addressing their need or assuming the need we hoped to find?
9. What financial, behavioral, contractual, or organizational switching costs apply?
10. Which decision rights and internal incentives affect adoption?
11. What happens before, during, and after the product's part of the workflow?
12. Who buys, uses, approves, and bears the risk, and where do their interests differ?
13. What would make a relevant customer recommend this to a colleague?
14. How do customers recognize and measure success?
15. What emotions accompany the problem, and what evidence supports that interpretation?

## Organizational health

1. When product and engineering disagree, how is the decision made and by whom?
2. How do delivery measures connect to customer or business outcomes?
3. Can individual contributors bring unwelcome information to leadership safely?
4. Do retrospectives lead to owned changes whose effects are reviewed?
5. What customer evidence and strategic constraints explain the roadmap?
6. How do we decide to continue, change, or stop work that is underperforming?
7. Do promotion decisions fairly consider contribution, context, outcomes, and collaboration?
8. What encourages people to share useful information or withhold it?
9. Which important assumption has become difficult to question?
10. If a key contributor left, what knowledge or continuity would be at risk?
11. Which work is exploring, expanding, or extracting value, and is the intended stage understood?
12. Does disagreement improve decisions, or does conduct prevent productive discussion?
13. How long do decisions take, and which delays have a useful purpose?
14. Where do individual incentives support or conflict with organizational goals?
15. Would I recommend this team to someone I respect, and what would I explain honestly?

## Stakeholders and influence

1. What responsibilities and incentives may shape this person's position, and what have they actually said?
2. Have I explained the proposal in terms relevant to their concerns as well as mine?
3. Which important conversation are we avoiding?
4. What objections might arise, and what evidence or options should we prepare?
5. What did an unexpected objection teach us about missing information or changed circumstances?
6. Is the disagreement about the task, trust, authority, or some combination?
7. What understanding and working relationship should the meeting leave behind?
8. What would make agreement reasonable, and what legitimate objection could remain?
9. Who needs decision authority, participation, or a clear delivery responsibility?
10. What risk concerns them most, and have we checked rather than assumed it?
11. Is there a specific issue on which their advice would be useful?
12. Could we say, “I've noticed friction around this decision. Can we discuss what is getting in the way?”
13. Am I giving leadership a clear account of risks, needs, and options?
14. What evidence suggests how they understand our team's work, and what needs clarification?
15. Is the obstacle evidence, reasoning, interests, the relationship, or several of these?

## AI and future capability

1. Is AI helping us reason and execute while we still evaluate its contribution responsibly?
2. If others have similar tools, what distinctive insight, judgment, data, access, or execution will matter?
3. What valuable need or insight remains if we replace AI with another method?
4. When is a summary sufficient, and which consequential claims require checking primary material?
5. What actions should the system be allowed to take, with what review and recovery?
6. What can go wrong, who bears the consequence, and how reversible is it?
7. Which decisions require accountable human judgment, and what assistance can be evaluated safely?
8. What customer evidence supports the AI feature beyond interest in the technology?
9. Which failures, costs, and correlated effects become consequential at scale?
10. Have relevant edge cases, misuse, adversarial behavior, and recovery paths been evaluated?
