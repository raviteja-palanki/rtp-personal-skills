## Framework lineage

[The Re-Wired Group’s discussion with Bob Moesta](https://therewiredgroup.com/circuit-breaker-podcast/unpacking-jobs-to-be-done/) describes progress through context, desired outcomes, forces, and hiring/firing criteria. Its [interview demonstration guide](https://therewiredgroup.com/news/blog-jtbd-interview-live-demonstration/) explicitly includes functional, emotional, and social motivations and a switching timeline. These are primary descriptions of the framework. They do not establish a universal numerical switching equation, that all emotional needs outrank functional performance, or that all non-adopters lack useful evidence.

The familiar drill/hole illustration encourages looking beyond a tool to its purpose. This revision does not rely on an unverified exact quotation or attribution to make that point.

## Workaround evidence

The [HBR publication record](https://hbr.org/2026/05/what-customer-workarounds-can-reveal-about-your-business-model) confirms Bohrer, Frankenberger, and Wincent’s article dated 21 May 2026. Its full argument here is drawn from the local research note, not claimed to have been fully available on the web.

The local note describes perceived mismatch, workaround engineering, and closure, with Michelin as the main case. It also distinguishes missing features from access, packaging, or pricing gaps. These are useful questions to investigate. The stronger claims that workarounds are pre-validated product-market fit, prove willingness to pay, or automatically demand a portfolio of business models exceed what a case-based account can establish.

A workaround may consume time without creating a viable paid market; an observed expense may come from a different budget than the proposed purchase. Consider whether an additional commercial model earns its complexity. Multiple examples within one article are not independent articles for a pattern-promotion threshold. This revision does not promote a ledger pattern or import the note’s unverified corporate growth figures.

## Product examples and prior numbers

Coding, writing, summarization, legal review, support drafting, and predictive maintenance examples describe possible needs rather than measured user motives. Claims that emotional-fit design explains Copilot’s success, makes users defend errors, triples support throughput, or determines industrial renewals were not established. The former anxiety multipliers, 90/10 effort split, thirty-day durability marker, five-interview minimum, and two-workaround threshold are not general evidence standards.

## Novel Insights applied

The ledger’s adoption distinctions separate declared use, actual use, and realized value. Its School of Rock account suggests that authorship can matter alongside economic benefit, but it is a company-reported case without a comparison group; co-authorship is not the only remedy for resistance. Its control-job check asks what a control actually verifies. That bounds the audit-defensibility example: a record can help reconstruct a decision without proving it was correct, transferring responsibility to a model, or ensuring a legal result.

The skill therefore treats additional needs as evidence-backed hypotheses, not hidden truths the researcher can infer from discomfort or silence. It carries actual capability, action/inaction consequences, and accountable ownership alongside emotional and social fit.
