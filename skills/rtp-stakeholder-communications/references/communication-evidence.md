# Communication evidence notes

This is a practical synthesis, not a validated communication scoring system. Sources were checked at different depths; those distinctions are explicit below. Numbers from an illustrative message are not research results.

## Relationship source and historical studies

**Aakash Gupta, “Stakeholder Management: Advanced Study,” Product Growth, 2 September 2026:** the user's full 3,741-word primary PDF text was read. It supplies the Apollo.io anecdote, network map, trust pyramid, relationship principles, and *Buy-In* discussion. The 8.75/10 and 6/10 ratings are the author's characterizations of one PM, not validated measures. Three quarters to visible improvement is one account. The claim that evidence chains are the only remaining credibility signal was a library extrapolation, not an established finding. The framework's numerical levels are not the library's AI autonomy levels.

[Cornell's account of Roghanizad and Bohns's 2017 research](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/news/about-ilr/gauging-influence) reports 45 participants asking ten strangers each to complete a survey and a 34-fold advantage for face-to-face requests over email. This is a scoped compliance result, not a multiplier for difficult workplace communication. Requesters misjudged channel effectiveness. The university-hosted paper download failed in this pass; no full-paper review is claimed. Ease of obtaining a yes is not automatically the same as good understanding or freely given agreement.

[Kelley, 1950, primary paper](https://eclass.uowm.gr/modules/document/file.php/NURED262/%CE%A0%CE%A1%CE%A9%CE%A4%CE%9F%CE%A4%CE%A5%CE%A0%CE%91%20%CE%91%CE%A1%CE%98%CE%A1%CE%91/Kelley%20the%20warm-cold%20variable%201950%20JP.pdf): methods and results were read. The study involved 55 male MIT students in three class sections, with two stimulus instructors. Warm/cold information was randomized within classes. Participation was reported as 56% versus 32%, but the author describes that relationship as significant only between the 5% and 10% levels. It is a limited historical experiment, not a universal 24-point workplace effect or proof that all traits respond equally.

[Thorndike, 1920, primary paper](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/1920-thorndike.pdf): the full five-page text was read. The .51 correlation is real but specifically concerns one commander's ratings of physique and intelligence for **137 aviation cadets**. The paper also reports .31 averaged across three other raters. These are correlations in ratings, not measured causal effects of appearance on intelligence or a modern rule for professional presentation. The practical lesson is to seek trait-relevant evidence and avoid general impressions contaminating judgment.

**Baumeister and colleagues, “Bad Is Stronger Than Good,” 2001:** the review is cited through Gupta in this pass; its original text was not newly reviewed. Retain the possibility that negative experiences weigh heavily without asserting that one mistake always outweighs every positive contribution or that this validates a perfection requirement.

The source also draws on Chris Voss's *Never Split the Difference*, Cohen and Bradford's *Influence Without Authority*, Kotter and Whitehead's *Buy-In*, and Cal Newport's failed-simulation idea. These support practical lenses. This pass does not claim a new full reading of those books or universal measured effects for their tactics.

## Leadership and channel sources in the user's research

- **Van Vugt, Sheng, and Andrews, “Are You Meeting the Needs of the People You Lead?”, HBR, 13 May 2026:** full primary text read. The authors report more than 3,500 working adults in the US, UK, and China and name their Fundamental Follower Needs Inventory. The six needs are a useful vocabulary; the article does not let an agent infer an individual's hidden need from a job title or establish that protection/fairness can always be supplied without trade-offs. A separate instrument validation dataset was not reviewed here.
- **Brooks and Atkins, “How the Best Leaders Shape Conversations,” HBR, 17 August 2026:** primary opening, goals, and roles passages checked. The authors report more than 1,000 participants, over 100 teams, and nearly 70 organizations. Their roughly fourfold association with rated effectiveness is not proof that a thirty-second intervention causes the improvement. The four role questions are retained in paraphrase; use decisions and actual follow-through as evidence.
- **Museum marketing case, HBR, July 2026:** the library records channel choice as a reported practice, with a campaign experiment whose results were not available in the prior synthesis. Do not infer that email always loses framing or that this source measured a general advantage for live communication.
- **“AI Experiments Need Domain Experts,” HBR, August 2026:** prior library review identified a two-site qualitative study over two years. Withdrawal at one site motivates checking participation; it does not establish that all status reports were green, all voluntary programs fail silently, or participation is the only early signal.
- **Lancefield's August 2026 writing advice:** outline-first is a proposed practice. Anchoring is a reason to inspect framing, not a ban on AI-assisted first drafts.
- **Sue Ashford's May 2026 issue-selling discussion:** practitioner guidance building on Lewin. The framework does not justify a universal ban on approaching an opponent directly.
- **Michael Watkins, July 2026 enterprise-leadership update:** seven shifts plus arguments about AI, geopolitics, and shorter development paths. No population estimate establishes that the executive in the room is underprepared.
- **Charles Duhigg, July 2026 candor discussion:** practical/emotional/social conversations and dissent practices are practitioner guidance. Equal airtime, public recognition, and silent reading are not uniquely sufficient mechanisms of psychological safety. The original library noted an outdated Amazon principle count; this skill needs the practice, not a volatile count.
- **May 2026 manager-review-bottleneck article:** practitioner accounts, not a measured comparison proving that extra reviewer capacity, summaries, or faster review never work.
- **Chris Lipp's June 2026 personal-power discussion:** meeting-role and moderation lenses are practitioner advice, with moderation attributed there to Maggie Neale. They do not establish that support tasks are low status or that facilitation always confers power.
- **Pamela Meyer, August 2026 informal-influence discussion:** five practical paths and chain analysis. Informal influence can matter without replacing formal authority. Accountability can require an appropriate decision record while confidentiality limits public detail.
- **July 2026 sovereign-AI article and Corporate Digital Responsibility framework:** the earlier source reports a vendor-commissioned survey of 1,928 executives. That does not prove boards only act on financial numbers or past incidents. Use concrete obligations and consequences without invented loss estimates.

Except for the explicitly identified primary readings, these entries preserve the existing library's source boundaries; they are not claims of fresh full-transcript or full-study review.

## Claim and example corrections

The three-audience example now keeps the same defined evidence set without saying every error is detectable beforehand. A 91% result on 400 binary cases corresponds to 364 successes; under independent-binomial assumptions its 95% Wilson interval is approximately 87.8–93.4%. An aggregate ECE of .04 cannot establish no overconfidence on errors.

For 200 equally weighted binary checks, 98.2% is not an attainable raw proportion; 196/200 is 98%. A different scoring or weighting method could produce 98.2%, but it must be defined.

The fictional enterprise examples distinguish precision from training-set performance, reuse from trust, pipeline touched from revenue lost, and correlation from causal attribution. Catalog growth of 23% makes the added portion 23/123, or about 18.7%, of the new total. Recovering 80% of a 14-percentage-point decline from 78% to 64% means returning to 75.2%. The summarizer case's reviewer acceptance is not relabeled factual accuracy, and its release remains subject to the actual ship decision.
