# Ownership and provider boundaries

Use these lenses selectively before or alongside the six checks in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). The cases identify questions worth asking; they do not assign sourcing by industry label alone.

## 0. Buying capability and creating advantage are different claims

The local note for Srivastava’s *3 Ways to Rethink Your Build-or-Buy Strategy* (HBR, June 2026) cites roughly 17,000 corporate transactions. The earlier skill summarized this as buying technology not producing innovation. That is too broad to carry without the study’s outcome, comparison, and identification method. Acquisition can provide capability, talent, rights, speed, or innovation; none automatically establishes durable advantage.

Identify what will create value after the transaction and how it will be maintained or improved. For parity, speed and reliability may be enough. The moat question should not displace a legitimate operating need.

## 1. Source at a useful task level

Agrawal’s outsourcing framework (HBR, June 2026) distinguishes four task patterns. Use them to compare options:

- **Routine, digital, high-volume:** internal automation and an AI-enabled vendor are both candidates. Compare quality, integration, control, and total cost.
- **Knowledge-heavy or sensitive:** protect context, data rights, and judgment. Those requirements can be met through internal, managed, or hybrid designs depending on the specifics.
- **Specialized but occasional:** external expertise may be more economical; check availability and the internal capability needed to direct it.
- **Regulated, consequential, or judgment-heavy:** establish accountable decisions and suitable expertise and controls. A particular combination of internal owner and external reviewer is one design, not a universal legal requirement.

A function can contain all four. Assess handoffs and end-to-end service as well as individual tasks; sourcing every task separately can create its own coordination cost.

## 2. Inspect a vendor’s actual basis of value

Stanton’s SaaS framework (HBR, May 2026) compares stored-answer retrieval with estimation and single-company data with pooled data. Retain the grid as a diagnostic:

| | One organization’s data | Shared or pooled data |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrieves stored information** | Compare the full workflow and service with the cost of replacement; a database interface may still include critical controls and integrations | Check whether the corpus is useful, lawfully usable, accessible elsewhere, and costly to reproduce |
| **Estimates an uncertain result** | Evaluate the model on actual local tasks and compare credible alternatives | Test whether pooling improves relevant performance, including difficult cases, and whether that advantage is durable |

The grid does not show that every stored-answer tool can be safely rebuilt by a nontechnical user or that pooled data makes a vendor irreplaceable. Common cases can create value; rare cases are not the only source. Public or purchasable data can be useful even when it is not exclusive. Compare the price with available substitutes and useful outcomes; do not infer a supplier’s justified price from an imagined expert-replacement cost alone.

## 3. Protect relevant operational history

The Lenovo supply-chain and Caterpillar data-transformation accounts describe accumulated operational information that supports their systems. The local Lenovo account includes a multi-year build and long manufacturing history. Treat these as case-specific observations, not evidence that every organization needs the same years of data or platform work.

Ask what the history explains, whether it still represents the task, whether comparable information can be obtained elsewhere, and what rights and expertise make it usable. A purchased model or product can operate over a customer-controlled corpus. The asset can remain valuable without training custom weights. Time can make some data difficult to reproduce, but acquisition, licensing, alternative signals, or a changed task may reduce that advantage.

## 4. Preserve a credible alternative and price supplier-specific investment

The local cost-shock discussion proposes that retiring internal expertise can weaken bargaining power. Test the actual alternatives, migration cost, substitute quality, and time to rebuild competence. There is no established universal moment at the second or third renewal when supplier leverage peaks. Retained practice is one hedge alongside alternative vendors, portability, contractual terms, and operational fallback.

The algorithmic-shopping article motivates a separate **hold-up hypothesis**: an investment useful mainly through one intermediary can create dependence after the investment is sunk. Inspect whether the asset is actually non-redeployable. Structured product data may also serve search, direct channels, customer service, or other intermediaries.

Price the specialized asset and any direct-channel or portability hedge together when the dependency warrants it. Do not prescribe building regardless of cost, assume exclusion is always worse than a toll, or claim every improvement inevitably raises the intermediary’s bargaining power. The asset may defend access, improve margin, do both, or be uneconomic. `rtp-moat-finder` and `rtp-marketing-to-ai-agents` address the competitive and distribution questions in more detail.

## 5. Verify jurisdiction for the actual workload

The local sovereign-AI account uses four provider archetypes: hyperscalers, nationally endorsed providers, AI-native specialists, and federated consortia. Its survey is described as 1,928 executives in 28 countries, with Accenture-affiliated authors; that context should travel with its findings.

Provider category alone does not determine legal eligibility, and jurisdiction does not replace engineering suitability. Check the relevant workload, parties, locations, data, service terms, residency or transfer conditions, export restrictions where applicable, and contractual requirements. Domestic or nonregulated use can still have relevant legal or procurement constraints. Seek a concrete interpretation for a material unresolved rule.

The AstraZeneca infrastructure split is a reported case, not proof that every similar workload legally requires the same architecture. A failover plan must name a permitted route; theoretical technical interchangeability is insufficient.

## 6. Distinguish an archive from continuing information

The Atlantic/OpenAI licensing discussion in the local library distinguishes existing content from a continuing stream. Its deal terms were not disclosed. Do not infer a one-time price for archives or recurring revenue for every flow.

A fixed archive can remain useful through repeated reference, licensed access, historical coverage, rights, and expertise. Copying does not necessarily make its value zero. Fresh material can decay quickly or be easy to replace. Either form can support infrastructure when the actual use, rights, quality, refresh cost, and alternative sources justify it. A training dataset and an accessible retrieval corpus also provide different capabilities.

The Novel Insights stock/flow entry is a hypothesis about changing demand and bargaining, not proof that model collapse makes every buyer continuously dependent on newly generated data.

## 7. Use partnerships to close a real delivery gap

The medical-center drug-discovery synthesis (Offodile and colleagues, HBR, April 2026) proposes five moves: portfolio discipline, technology integrated into discovery, strategic partnerships, financing through later development, and global collaboration. Preserve the need for domain expertise in defining questions and judging consequences.

The authors describe an incomplete synthesis rather than an institution proven to have implemented all five. Funding through grants, philanthropy, operating resources, or investment depends on the situation; none is categorically unable to support later stages.

A partnership can reduce internal engineering requirements while adding integration, oversight, contracting, privacy, IP, and coordination work. Price both. “Governance is the build” is a reminder to count retained responsibilities, not an accounting identity saying partnership never saves engineering cost. Collaborate within applicable constraints and define what each party can use or retain.

## 8. Choose the organizational level that has the needed asset

The Apollo case (HBR, June 2025) describes industry assessment, company diligence, an implementation plan developed before close, and senior commitment to tools and talent. Borrow these checks where they fit; industry and company analysis can be iterative rather than a mandatory one-way sequence.

The central team’s stated aim of company self-sufficiency is one operating model. A deliberately funded, accountable shared service can also be appropriate. Define the handoff or ongoing service relationship instead of assuming every permanent central capability is harmful dependence.

A portfolio-wide purchasing corpus illustrates information available above the individual-company level. Confirm rights to combine and process it, and compare the value with outside alternatives. Such visibility is one possible central advantage, not the only thing a parent organization can build.

## Provider reuse: current primary check and evidence limits

The September 2026 local HBR note, *Is Your AI Training the Competition?*, proposes feedback, synthetic reuse, and structural learning as distinct concerns. Preserve the questions while checking the actual service. The claim that ordinary usage necessarily trains a provider’s general product was not established; the structural channel is explicitly a hypothesis without a cited incident.

As a concrete primary check, [Anthropic’s commercial terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms), inspected September 13, 2026 and displaying an effective date of June 17, 2025, distinguish customer content from voluntary service feedback. They prohibit model training on customer content under those terms and contain a separate feedback provision. [Anthropic’s training FAQ](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996868-is-my-data-used-for-model-training) describes the commercial default and possible training use when feedback or permission is provided. Those distinctions matter; they do not prove that all logs or corrections are unrestricted feedback. Verify the agreement and feature used, including any negotiated terms.

This check does not establish identical terms for OpenAI, every Anthropic deployment route, consumer accounts, or future versions. A provider’s own synthetic output is not automatically exempt from obligations attached to the interaction that produced it. Consumer content is not “fair game” without regard to settings, terms, and applicable rights.

The earlier company-subscription versus personal-use percentages also have different denominators and limited provenance. They do not quantify the effect of a new restriction. Investigate whether approved tools meet the actual need, and map exposure before choosing a control. A sandbox’s effectiveness depends on what it isolates; a truly isolated local route and a sandbox making remote API calls have different data flows.
