# Research boundaries and planning examples

This reference preserves useful examples and explains which claims require local validation. The working procedure is in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

## Plan the study from the question

Earlier examples proposed 15–25 people for a two-to-four-week field study; 150 outputs with fifty per use case for quality; one hundred people over four-to-eight weeks for trust; five hundred per arm for an A/B test; and thirty people with five weekly interactions for calibration. These are possible plans, not general minimums or evidence of adequate power. The necessary sample depends on the claim, variation, precision or effect of interest, assignment unit, dependence, missing data, and subgroup analysis. Qualitative adequacy also depends on scope and information quality.

The same applies to ten-to-fifteen expectation interviews and follow-ups at weeks two and eight. Fifty weekly users is not an eligibility requirement. A small, well-scoped study may resolve a meaningful uncertainty; a large study can still fail to identify the intended effect.

## Quality and time examples

The previous quality conditions of 50%, 65%, 75%, 85%, and 95% require an explicit rubric and denominator. They do not form a standard experimental scale. The domain thresholds—60% for coding, 40–50% for creative work, 70% for support, and 90–95% for healthcare—were not supported as general acceptance or safety standards. Choose relevant dimensions and measure outcomes, user response, and consequences separately.

Cost of error, ability to detect it, and alternatives are useful considerations. Multiplying them without defined scales and a justified model does not produce a valid minimum-quality formula.

Trust need not follow a sequence of week-one caution, week-two-to-three growth, week-four stability, and week-five-to-eight collapse. Duration should cover the task cycles and experiences the claim concerns. Investigate attrition and verification changes rather than assigning them a universal cause.

Earlier 2–3%, 5%, and 10–15% revalidation bands also lacked a general basis and clear units. Specify what change could affect the particular finding, including a changed tail despite an unchanged mean. Distinguish percentages from percentage points.

## Trust and mental models

[Lee and See (2004)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392), also indexed in [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15151155/), reviews trust and reliance in automation and their context. The primary abstract supports these distinctions, not a fixed multi-week curve. [Google PAIR’s mental-model guidance](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/mental-models/) is a design resource, not a validated threshold schedule.

The earlier citation “Gero et al., Designing for AI Uncertainty (CHI)” was not sufficiently identified to verify. Do not treat that title as a checked source without locating the actual publication.

## Simulated participants: validity is task-specific

Local HBR research notes on AI-assisted innovation warn that simulated customers may react to framing or interpret price as a quality cue. Preserve that possibility as a hypothesis to test; do not conclude that all simulated customers are optimistic or that all simulation is invalid.

Primary counterevidence comes from [Park and colleagues, arXiv version 3, June 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v3). Its abstract reports agents grounded in interviews or surveys from 1,052 Americans. Held-out survey performance is expressed relative to participants’ own test–retest consistency; the reported 83%, 82%, and 86% are not raw accuracy percentages. This supports investigating grounded simulation on validated tasks. It does not establish accuracy for a new product’s price, demand, or adoption forecast. The abstract was reviewed; these results are not presented here as independently replicated market validation.

## Novel Insights: carry the mechanism with its limits

- **Measurement grain:** task-specific follow-up can make a broad concern actionable. Broad measures can still validly measure mood, satisfaction, or another broad construct. Confidential interviews, tailored quantification, and joint interpretation are useful options rather than a required sequence.
- **Interdependence claims:** cross-sectional self-report may leave causal direction and component necessity unresolved. Ceiling-based categories can discard information. Neither fact makes every survey or cutoff useless, and objective outcomes alone do not prove a dependency.
- **Information diversity:** the local retrieval example reports novice/expert semantic cluster counts of 1/2 under standard search and 2/5 under exploratory retrieval. The initial groups are not equal. Counts alone do not establish significance, power, or a general advantage for generated cross-domain prompts. The reported laboratory and field samples, 104 and 245, are not enough to infer power without design and effect information.
- **Participant state:** shifts, workload, interruptions, sleep, and time-related conditions may change the experience. The proposed daily sequence of creativity, focus, agreeableness, and evening exploration is not a validated schedule for recruiting or synthesizing research. Measure relevant conditions without collecting unnecessary sensitive information.
