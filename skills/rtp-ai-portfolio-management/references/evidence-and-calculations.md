## Framework attribution

The project-pipeline and portfolio-dashboard approach draws on Faisal Hoque, Erik Nelson, Tom Davenport, and Paul Scade, [Manage Your AI Investments Like a Portfolio](https://store.hbr.org/product/manage-your-ai-investments-like-a-portfolio/H091DR), published January 21, 2026. The local account connects the approach to Northrop Grumman, PepsiCo, and U.S. Army work and describes Lloyds Banking Group’s GenAI Control Tower. Those implementations illustrate an approach rather than establish a comparative success rate.

OPEN names four stages—Outline, Partner, Experiment, Navigate. This skill can use another stage vocabulary without changing the need for explicit commitments and a view of dependencies. Ravi’s 3E framework is a separate decision lens: Explore, Exploit, or Exit, introduced April 5, 2026. Buy/Hold/Sell describes resource allocation, and the five investment types describe how value may arise. Keep the four frameworks distinct in the review record.

## Three historical studies measure different things

**ISG, 2025.** The [primary report](https://isg-one.com/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf) describes 400 senior AI decision-makers profiling their three most-funded use cases, producing 1,200 cases. The [report page](https://isg-one.com/state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report-2025) reports 31% in production, one in four achieving expected growth ROI, and 50% achieving expected efficiency gains. This is a selected set of prioritized investments and self-reported expectations, not a random sample of every AI initiative or a universal probability of failure. Its primary guidance also cautions against both waiting for an all-enterprise data transformation and creating isolated pipelines for every immediate need.

**Project NANDA, 2025.** The original skill cites secondary reporting of a preliminary study: 150 leader interviews, a 350-employee survey, and 300 public deployments, with a widely repeated 95% no-measurable-P&L-impact claim. These populations and measures should not be collapsed into one representative trial-success denominator. A current originating primary report was not located in this editorial pass; retain the claim as unverified historical context rather than using it to calibrate a gate. The earlier ~33% versus ~67% sourcing figures would imply an approximate twofold ratio, not a threefold ratio, even if measured comparably. They do not by themselves identify a causal advantage for buying, and “no measured P&L effect” is not interchangeable with failed deployment, no learning, or no local benefit.

**PwC, 2026.** The [primary release, April 13, 2026](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-performance-study.html), reports a study of 1,217 senior executives across 25 sectors and a concentration of 74% of reported AI economic value in 20% of organizations. It reports associations with business-model reinvention, autonomy, and governance; the earlier skill retains multipliers of 2.6, 2.8, 1.7, and 1.5 for the described practices. These survey comparisons are not an independent audit of each company’s financial gain or proof that the management practices caused the gap. Selection, maturity, investment, market, and other factors can differ.

The studies do not converge on one base rate: growth expectations, production, P&L impact, and concentration of reported value are different outcomes. They motivate clear hypotheses and review; they neither prove that most value gaps are measurement artifacts nor establish that every gap is a management failure. Technology and product fit remain possible causes.

## Five investment types: examples and limits

The taxonomy comes from Baba Prasad’s June 2026 HBR article, identified in the local library as *The 5 Types of AI Investment—and How to Capture Their Value*. It is a practitioner classification, not an experimentally validated allocation formula.

| Type | Original illustration retained | What the illustration does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive parity | Bank of America’s Erica, with three billion cumulative interactions in the dated source account | Interactions are not financial return or proof that parity produces no return |
| Option value | Moderna’s mChat and a reported 750-plus custom GPTs | GPT count is not the value of the options or proof of the ability to use them well |
| Unique integration | Amazon forecasting connected to robotics | Integration is not automatically unique, nonportable, or defensible |
| Data flywheels and lock-in | John Deere See & Spray | A useful AI product does not by itself demonstrate a learning loop, exclusive data rights, or durable switching barriers |
| Organizational capability building | Walmart’s Element platform and a reported roughly 50,000-person reskilling effort | Participation in training does not establish acquired or retained capability |

These historical counts were not freshly re-audited in this pass. Verify the source, date, and exact measure before using them in a current business case. The author’s estimate that more than 70% of observed Fortune 500 AI spending falls into types 1 and 2 has an unstated sample; it is not a population allocation benchmark.

An initiative may have several value mechanisms or change type. Separate claims and outcomes rather than inventing a single allocation formula. Tactical spending can produce benefits and be essential; strategic spending can fail. A short payback test may undervalue a long-lived option, but a longer evaluation period does not make that option worthwhile automatically.

## Shared foundations and practical governance

**Caterpillar.** The local MIT CISR/MIT SMR case, *Data Transformation Is the CEO’s Business*, connects business outcomes, named ownership, sustained platform investment, stakeholder input, and dependent AI work. It also describes AI-assisted anomaly correction. Preserve the sequencing question and the measurement chain from asset fitness to use to realized outcome. Services-revenue growth is not attributable to the data program alone. A single case does not establish that an enterprise platform must always precede all AI work, that revenue is the only valid target, or that naming an owner prevents duplicate systems.

**Lloyds.** The local portfolio article describes a cross-functional forum that prioritizes use cases, allocates resources, balances near- and long-term work, and coordinates risk, legal, ethics, bias, and security review before production. A centralized playbook and explicit decision rights support that model. The case illustrates responsibilities worth assigning; it does not require every team to create a control tower or prove that a committee guarantees sound decisions.

**Expert participation.** The local two-site, two-year study reports more than 80% of domain experts withdrawing at a failing site, without a clear denominator for that figure. The useful question is whether a required contribution remains available and why it changes. Treat staffing and participation as operational dependencies, not an automatic stop whenever a percentage falls.

**Capacity.** The Morris practitioner account emphasizes postponing competing work, clarifying decisions, reallocating resources, and protecting thinking time. These can create capacity. Hiring and sourcing can also help when the bottleneck and coordination costs permit. Requiring a named cancellation for every addition would reject work that fits genuinely spare or newly created capacity.

## Sequencing: preserve the context of a forecast

The Novel Insights entry compares Spataro’s February 2024 discussion, published in January 2025, with a later November 2025 account of agents in internal processes. It contrasts a roadmap from individual assistants to function-level automation to firm-level agents with the consequences of customer-facing errors.

Carry the decision-relevant distinction: breadth, capability, and acceptable consequences are different variables. Compare actual tasks and actions, not customer-facing versus back-office labels alone. The later reported project account does not validate a universal three-stage sequence or establish that learning pace is predictable while deployment surfaces are unknowable. Consequences can often be investigated before production; uncertainty can justify a bounded test rather than a scope-based shortcut.

The same ledger links standing technical and organizational capabilities to portfolio value. A parity classification does not imply a zero holding period or mandatory renting, and an integration or flywheel label does not require platform-first development. Evaluate the actual horizon, dependencies, rights, reuse, and economics.

## Financial interpretation and a worked check

Use consistent periods, currencies, inflation assumptions, and relevant incremental cash flows. Account for implementation and recurring operations, support, review, changes in working capital where material, and any exit obligations. Show uncertainty and the mechanism by which task-level improvement becomes a financial benefit. Follow the organization’s finance conventions for taxes and discount rates.

Consider an illustrative initiative with an initial $100,000 outlay and $60,000 annual net cash inflow for two years, after recurring costs. At a 10% annual discount rate:

```text
NPV = -100,000 + 60,000 / 1.10 + 60,000 / 1.10²
    ≈ $4,132
```

Using gross benefits before operating costs in place of the $60,000 would overstate value. Ordinary payback is approximately 1.67 years only if cash inflow accrues evenly within those years; with year-end receipts, recovery occurs at the second receipt. An ROI figure needs its cost convention and period before it can be compared with another.

[ACCA’s investment-appraisal guidance](https://www.accaglobal.com/hk/en/student/exam-support-resources/prodipsust-study-resources/technical-articles/investment-appraisal.html) covers cash-flow-based appraisal. Its [NPV/IRR comparison](https://www.accaglobal.com/content/dam/acca/global/PDF-students/acca/f9/exampapers/fm-2018-sepdec-sample-a.pdf) explains why scale, nonconventional flows, and mutually exclusive choices can make IRR rankings misleading. Use a comparable horizon and analyze the incremental choice under the actual constraints.

The skill’s 1,875 hours × $125 example equals $234,375. That is valued capacity under the stated rate, not automatically cash released or annual profit. If the same saved hours support both a platform business case and several downstream projects, reconcile attribution before totaling the portfolio.

## Replace templates with explicit local choices

The earlier 50/30/20 horizon mix and three-to-six, six-to-eighteen, and eighteen-plus-month bands are illustrative. Count, spend, and staffing shares are different portfolios. A high score is a visible judgment, not a probability of success. Define rating anchors, weights, and direction; a resource requirement should not earn more points merely because it is larger.

The earlier stage map omitted Explore after Stage 3 and during operation. Restore it when a specific unanswered question warrants another bounded investment. A pivot inherits relevant evidence only within its original scope; it still needs its own problem and commitment decision. A justified exit without an adjacent pivot is a complete decision.
