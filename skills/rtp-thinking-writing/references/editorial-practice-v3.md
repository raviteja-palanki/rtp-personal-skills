# Editorial practice from original readings

Use this companion for major researched writing and teaching packages. The lessons below come from reading the actual local articles and comparing their argumentative work with the S01 v02 defects. They are editorial interpretations, not experimental evidence that a particular prose style improves learning.

## 1. Start with a useful distinction, then show its consequences

**Reading:** Faisal Hoque, Tom Davenport and Paul Scade, *AI transformation requires redesigning work, not cutting roles*, HBR, 28 August 2026. Read the complete local original.

The article questions a decision unit: whole roles are too coarse when AI changes particular tasks. It then follows the consequences into coordination, tacit operational knowledge, review, redeployment and reversibility. The useful craft is the movement from an understandable management mistake to a more precise way of deciding.

**Apply it:** When introducing a concept, explain the distinction that improves the reader's judgment before naming more frameworks. In S01, a model's output, a workflow's completion and a business result are different levels. Their relationship needs explanation, rather than three slogans.

**Do not import the article's certainty wholesale.** Statements about inherently human work or the causes of workforce outcomes still need evidence. The editorial structure can be useful even where the source's claim needs qualification.

**Before:** “Human oversight is essential for trustworthy AI.”

**After:** “A reviewer can improve the decision only if they can see the relevant evidence, have time to examine it and can change the proposed action. Otherwise, adding review may create delay without reliably catching errors.”

The revision names the mechanism and the conditions. It does not claim that every action needs human approval.

## 2. Give a management claim an operating mechanism

**Reading:** Alex Singla, Alexander Sukharevsky, Eric Lamarre, Kate Smaje and Robert Levin, *The new management playbook for AI: How to move faster and create more value*, McKinsey, August 2026. Read all eight local PDF pages.

The article connects company-level value with common capabilities and operating ownership. Its named cases give the reader something to inspect: journey teams and enterprise platforms at DBS, or central assets and local adaptation at Freeport. The case does work when it explains how an organisational choice affects delivery.

**Apply it:** A role definition should explain why the organisation would fund the role, which user it serves, what decision it owns and what result would justify that expense. “Own the roadmap” is a task description. It does not explain the role's value.

**Before:** “Platform PMs own reusable AI infrastructure.”

**After:** “A platform PM makes recurring capabilities dependable and usable across teams. The investment earns its place when teams avoid duplicated work and can deliver their own products more effectively; an abstraction that creates more integration work needs reconsideration.”

**Boundary:** The article selected successful transformations. Their financial results cannot establish a typical AI return or prove that the named practices caused the results. Its organisational examples do not establish employees' formal PM titles.

## 3. Treat seams between tasks as part of the story

**Reading:** James Mortemore, with contributions from Subu Narayanan and editing by Jo Stichbury, *Escaping AI pilot purgatory: From POC to P&L*, QuantumBlack, 27 August 2026. Read the complete local two-long-page PDF.

The article ties adoption, workflow fit, engineering ownership, domain knowledge and reusable assets together. Its useful insight is that these dependencies reinforce one another. A list of seven lessons would preserve the headings while losing the mechanism.

**Apply it:** Explain how one decision changes the next person's work. A faster draft creates value only if it reduces the work needed to reach the decision; a shared capability creates value only if another team can use it. Follow the handoff, not merely the output.

**Counterexample:** Some projects are held back by model performance. “The model is never the bottleneck” would overstate the source and give bad advice. Diagnose the actual constraint.

**Workflow improvement:** Capture reusable reasoning in a short source card and usable instrument. Keeping thousands of words in an unread digest is not the same as making the learning available at the next decision.

## 4. Convert descriptive research into questions before recommendations

**Reading:** Marc Zao-Sanders, *How people are really using AI in 2026*, HBR, 1 June 2026. Read both local PDF pages.

The material makes everyday uses concrete, including the tension between assistance and surrendering independent thought. The data is social listening, not a representative measure of all users.

**Apply it:** Use an observed pattern to formulate a question about the learner's context. Do not turn a popular use into a universal strategic recommendation. A widely discussed behaviour may reveal a useful job, a fashionable experiment or a reporting bias.

**Before:** “Everyone is using AI to write, so companies must redesign content teams.”

**After:** “Writing assistance can reveal where people struggle to turn an idea into usable text. The product question is whether the assistance improves the completed work, including the thinking and review the next person still has to do.”

## 5. Advance a case; change examples when the question changes

The v02 defect was not the use of support. The familiar case made machinery accessible. The defect was repeated setup and treating several important questions as variants of one story.

In v03, the support case progresses through three beats: the promise and stakeholders; development of classification; operation and economics. Other examples explain other decisions. Shopping is useful for applied design; model APIs for platform economics; industrial operations for deployment ownership; a policy dispute for service accountability.

Before adding a return to an earlier case, complete: **“The reader learns something new here because…”** If the answer is only another framework label, revise the section or use a better example.

This is not a fixed three-beat rule for every artifact. A short answer may need one example; an extended case study may need many returns. The requirement is useful progression.

## 6. Make supporting artifacts test the teaching

A note can sound convincing while its exercise exposes missing reasoning. Team E originally introduced extra review capacity, allowing students to answer “add people and count the cost.” The revised card keeps the disagreement: finance wants review removed; operations lacks evidence for that scope.

Students now need to identify the decision owner, specify the evidence needed, address the cost constraint and consider narrowing or stopping expansion. The private guide must accept more than one defensible response. The slide plan must preserve the same card, numbers and authority assumptions.

This transfers to other topics. An evaluation exercise needs cases that can contradict an average. A pricing exercise needs costs and customer value on compatible units. An ethics exercise needs a consequential choice and affected stakeholders, rather than a list of approved principles.

## 7. Learn from strong publications without adopting their filler

Read for the argumentative work: concrete distinction, causal connection, selective example and an operational implication. Do not imitate prestigious publications' familiar urgency formulas, dramatic generalisations or unverified statistics.

A good editorial question is: **If the source name were hidden, would this paragraph still explain something?** If not, replace borrowed authority with a mechanism and appropriate evidence.

## 8. Check the artifact at its moment of use

The S01 worksheet revision exposed a delivery failure: a clear explanation in the post-class note cannot help a student during the activity. A case also needs an explicit location and an assigned reading path. The repair is to make the activity self-contained and reduce the required response to four decisions, while keeping a compact reference available. This is an availability and usability rule, not a rule that every worksheet needs to reproduce a textbook.
