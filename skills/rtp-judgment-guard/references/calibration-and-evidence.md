# Judgment Guard — Calibration and evidence notes

These notes preserve the original skill's numerical examples and research leads while separating what they establish from what they merely suggest. The main skill's design should use evidence for the actual task, role, population, and time period.

## Unsupported timelines and thresholds

The previous skill presented a consistent progression from adoption in months 0–3, through complacency in 4–6 and delegation in 7–12, to dependency in 13–18. It also stated that restoring judgment costs two or three times as much as losing it, and elsewhere that loss over eighteen months takes nine to fifteen months to rebuild. The source file provides no adequate longitudinal evidence for these rates; the claims also mix time and cost inconsistently.

Retain the stages only as a **hypothetical risk narrative**. Capability loss is not established by the calendar, and a six-week project can still expose immediate anchoring or poor review. Begin useful assessment early enough for the decision, not because month one or month twelve has a universal meaning.

| Earlier prescription or signal | Correct use |
|---|---|
| No more than 80% of decisions through one tool; rotate every four weeks | Possible practice schedule to test, subject to safe task exposure, workload, and learning needs. |
| Monthly calibration using 10–20 cases | A potential learning exercise; estimate precision and coverage before treating it as a reliable performance rate. |
| No override in six months, below 1%, or below 5% after six months | Investigate with the underlying error rate and task mix; not proof of disengagement. |
| Could the person perform at 70% if AI vanished? | Define the needed fallback tasks and standard explicitly; 70% may be inadequate or irrelevant. |
| Human performance flat while AI improves | Relative gap, not demonstrated loss of human ability. |
| Calibration falls 5% or reasoning complexity falls 30% | Unvalidated original alert examples. Define units, baseline, uncertainty, and the relation to meaningful outcomes. |
| Every disagreement stopped, none averaged | Appropriate only where the defined boundary requires it. Other tasks may use justified adjudication or aggregation. |
| About 10% of human time reserved for checks | A planning assumption to cost and test, not an established sufficient dose. |

## Cost calculations in the original concept guide

The earlier costs were arithmetically inconsistent. With the stated inputs:

```text
4–6 hours per specialist per month × 20 specialists × 12 months
= 960–1,440 person-hours/year

At $200/person-hour: $192,000–$288,000/year.
If 4–6 hours instead means the whole team's monthly total,
the annual total is 48–72 person-hours, or $9,600–$14,400.
Neither interpretation yields the old 200 hours/$40,000.

20–30 seconds per decision × 200 decisions/week
= 4,000–6,000 seconds/week = about 66.7–100 minutes/week.
Against a 40-hour week, that is about 2.8%–4.2%,
not the earlier 40–60 minutes or 15%–20% claim.
```

These are illustrative workload and rate assumptions, not recommendations about professional staffing. Add preparation, debriefing, coverage, and administration if required. Automatic documentation is not free; it may reduce typing while introducing review and maintenance work. Comparing the cost with a hypothetical $500K expert does not establish an ROI without a credible avoided-loss or capability benefit.

## Evidence for complementarity and learning

**Vaccaro, Almaatouq, and Malone, 2024:** [When combinations of humans and AI are useful](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06087), published in *Nature Human Behaviour*, synthesizes over 100 experimental studies. Combinations performed worse on average than the better standalone alternative, with heterogeneous results across tasks. This directly bounds the original assertion of automatic synergy. It does not mean that all human oversight is harmful or resolve any specific legal requirement. Test the relevant division of labor and baselines.

**Bastani and colleagues:** [Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) examines learning with different AI tutoring designs and later unaided performance. It demonstrates that research on skill acquisition under AI assistance exists, so the original “nobody has measured” assertion should not be carried forward. It does not establish a universal effect on professional expertise or a lifelong cohort trajectory. No numerical effect from that study is used as a workplace planning constant here.

**Center for Creative Leadership:** the [70–20–10 framework](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/70-20-10-rule/) emphasizes challenging work, developmental relationships, and formal learning. CCL describes its use as a guideline rather than a fixed formula. The original inference that automating entry-level work removes 90% of development does not follow. Examine which experiences are removed and what replaces them.

**Gu, Li, and Zhu:** [Should Humans Be in the Loop? Human-AI Collaboration Paradox and Automation Cliffs](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6417798) is the theoretical working-paper lead used for the “dead zone.” The original describes reduced reviewer effort under particular incentives and model-performance conditions. The full paper was not retrieved in this editorial check; do not claim that it proves every reviewer rationally disengages at a universal performance bar. A theoretical model's optimum depends on its assumptions. Falling overrides and stable outputs cannot by themselves identify the mechanism.

## Study and practitioner sources retained from the original

Unless a primary check is described above or below, these entries preserve the supplied skill and research notes' attribution. They are not newly verified effect estimates.

### Framing, ownership, and explanation avoidance

- **BCG framing trial:** the local account reports **1,261 participants**, an employee-like AI framing associated with roughly a **nine-point** accountability decrease and **18% fewer** errors detected. Verify scale, denominator, treatment, and task before quoting a transferable effect. Naming an AI as a colleague is not enough to infer the effect in a new workflow. Okposo's **“Accountability Must Be Chosen, Not Mandated,” HBR, 29 April 2026** supplies the mindset/meaning/mechanisms practitioner framing, not a separate replication of the trial.
- **AI gravity, MIT Sloan Executive Academy, June 2026:** an argued account of effort, competition, and peer behavior. Its original uncited empirical figure was already marked unusable; no replacement number is supplied here.
- **Alex Chan:** the primary abstract for **“Preference for Explanations: Case of Explainable AI,” HBS Working Paper 26-028**, describes participants acting as loan officers on real **$10,000** loans and varying incentives and explanation framing. A later version circulates as [NBER Working Paper 35240](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35240). The local HBR/HBS account reports **2,512 participants**, **80%** seeking predictions versus about **46%** seeking explanations, about **20%** more explanation avoidance with repayment-linked bonuses, and an avoidance rise of over ten percentage points to **23%** under a bias warning. Those figures should retain their original conditions and units; the headline percentages are not necessarily interchangeable denominators. The roughly six-point override increase concerns the study's decisions and is not a universal error-correction rate. The abstract also describes a separate contingent-reasoning task, so motive is not the only possible mechanism.

### Capability, roles, and handoffs

- **Thinkslop census, June 2026:** **12,637 use cases** from roughly **50,000 records across six platforms**, according to the local account. Social listening can identify reported patterns; it is not a probability sample or a measure of cognitive prevalence.
- **Renieris and colleagues, MIT SMR, 12 May 2026:** the **84% panel-poll** figure is opinion from a particular panel, not a population estimate. **Liu and Kovács, “Big Tech's Looming Capability Crisis,” HBR, 2 June 2026** motivates the training-pipeline concern. The original roughly **$570K** radiologist pay and **8,000** Meta cuts are contextual source leads, not evidence that a particular learning mechanism caused a loss.
- **Dan Wang, HBR IdeaCast, 2026:** process knowledge as a distinct asset is a conceptual account; no retention effect size is established by it.
- **School of Rock, HBR Cold Call, June 2026:** different instructor responses to a standardized library suggest role- and experience-sensitive design. Co-authorship is a reported intervention, not proof that incentive redesign cannot help.
- **Holweg and Davenport, “Don't Let AI Slop Muck Up Your Company's Processes,” HBR, 16 June 2026:** a mechanism account of knowledge degradation. The local case of a Deloitte Australia report worth about **AUD 440,000** with roughly **20 fabricated references** is not a measured handoff-decay rate. A factual case correction does not require a formula for cumulative loss.
- **“Your Talent Strategy Has to Keep Up with Your AI Transformation,” HBR, June 2026:** uses the learning-framework analogy; see the CCL limit above.
- **“AI Transformation Requires Redesigning Work, Not Cutting Roles,” HBR, 28 August 2026:** the original cites a 2025 Korn Ferry survey with **41%** reporting reduced management layers, partly attributed to AI, and a separate reported survey of **600 HR leaders** after AI-related layoffs with **55%** saying more oversight was needed than expected. The latter pollster was not identified in the skill. These are reported responses, not causal evidence about every management layer.
- **Pamela Meyer, HBR IdeaCast, August 2026:** the shortcut-versus-depth analogy extends the question to interpersonal skill. One practitioner's observation does not establish measured interpersonal atrophy.

### Pre-emption, disclosure, and friction

- **Blangeois and Roulet, “'Leadership Drift' Is Stalling Your AI Strategy,” HBR, August 2026:** one professional-services organization, qualitative interviews and convened workshops. Private/public changes motivate a suppression hypothesis; they can also reflect deliberation, measurement, or research-setting effects. Do not treat private views as ground truth merely because they differ from public ones.
- **Lancefield, “Don't Let AI Flatten Your Leadership Style,” HBR, August 2026:** four anonymized coaching cases. The margin-summary vignette motivates a framing question, not a measured decline in leadership ability. The audience-size exposure idea is the corpus's inference, not a source effect.
- **Duhigg on Amazon's memo ritual, HBR IdeaCast, 2026:** a practitioner account of discussion ordering. It does not establish that a mandatory field always creates fabricated prose or that an empty optional field is reliable reassurance.
- **“Don't Let AI Destroy the Skills That Make Your Company Competitive,” HBR, April 2026:** the four-capability list and people/rules/relationships lens are practitioner proposals without measured effects in the supplied account.
- **Mithu Storoni, *Hyperefficient*, HBR discussion, May 2026:** the three-gear account applies an arousal metaphor. No effect sizes or complete reference for the air-traffic-control example appear in the skill. Do not state that high pressure guarantees rubber-stamping or that low-action monitoring has only a physiological cause.
- **“Research: When Consumers Have More Control Over Ads, They Respond Better,” HBR, June 2026:** the original cites **9–15%** greater attention and **8–17%** lower irritation under matched controls. This is consumer-advertising evidence. Equivalence of particular ad controls does not establish a domain-general equivalence between light and heavy professional review; the three-variable review-sizing rule was the corpus's extrapolation.
- **Sudakov and Furr; Sloan and Glaser; Choe and Goldsmith, 2026:** the original draws practitioner ideas about advisory networks, directing work, and perspective. Distinguish useful generated critique from independent empirical or stakeholder evidence. The claim that an AI critic can only recall objections already anticipated by the prompt-writer is too strong.

### Checkpoints and live challenges

- **Duncan and Anderson, “Help Employees Get Better, Not Just Faster, with AI,” HBR, 15 June 2026:** state-first comparison and plausible-error scrutiny are conceptual proposals. Its **90%/10%** and **two-weeks-to-one-hour** figures are illustrative. The Dell'Acqua/Mollick “jagged frontier” study, HBS 24-013, is a separate research source, not validation of every checkpoint in this skill.
- **MIT SMR checkpoint accounts, 2026:** batching and the “why are you following” prompt are practitioner leads. The banking example is described as an ongoing unpublished pilot with an unspecified population. A post-recommendation written reason is not the same intervention as a pre-output judgment.
- **McCall, Wolfberg, Bilsborough, and Pruna, “How Elite Sports Coaches Make High-Pressure Decisions,” HBR, July–August 2026:** a qualitative account involving **11 coaches**, not a measured repair effect or a reason to delay incident containment.
- **Gill, “Rule Breaking in Organizations: An Integrative Review,” Academy of Management Annals, DOI 10.5465/annals.2023.0170:** the original describes **250+ studies across four decades**. The typology helps inquiry; it is not an automatic disciplinary or legal rule. The cited **ECI 2023** survey of more than **70,000 employees across 42 countries**, with **65%** reporting observed misconduct versus **60%** in 2020, is a separate disclosure-context statistic, not validation of the proposed candor matrix.
- **“How the Best Leaders Shape Conversations,” HBR, August 2026:** the local account describes more than one hundred teams and the rank of a second-order-candor item. The two-item matrix is a corpus construction and has not been validated as the diagnostic presented here.
- **Mass General Brigham scribe account, HBR, August 2026:** roughly **800 self-selected clinicians** in the reported pilot, without a controlled comparison in the source account. Role-specific effects on attending practice and resident observation remain hypotheses; a stylistic mismatch in a note does not establish atrophy.
- **“4 Steps to Transform the 'Middle Office' with AI,” HBR, August 2026:** the claim that expert interrogation preserves expertise is asserted rather than demonstrated. Asking whether an expert answering a hundred exception questions a year remains calibrated is a proposed study, not an established sufficient dose.
- **KPMG/UT Austin early-career account, HBR, July 2026:** **523 participants** at one site, unpublished in the supplied account. It reports **24.1% apprentices**, **25.8% delegators**, and **50.1% amplifiers**, with different performance relative to an AI-only baseline. Preserve the distinction between described profiles and randomized interventions. The reported association with criteria-before-output supports testing sequence; it does not prove sequence alone caused the differences or that foundational skill never matters.
- **McGrath, “The Power of Strategic Centering,” HBR, August 2026:** five center types form a conceptual strategy framework. The customer-friction/reviewer-friction distinction is the corpus's inference; evaluate what each form of effort accomplishes.

## Regulatory citation correction

The original treated CFPB Circular **2023-03** as current authority. The CFPB's [withdrawn-guidance record](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/guidance/withdrawn-guidance/) lists it as withdrawn on **12 May 2025**. Preserve it only as historical context. Withdrawal of that guidance does not by itself establish that the underlying statutory obligations disappeared.

The original also grouped GDPR and the EU AI Act into a broad explanation/oversight claim. Determine the applicable jurisdiction, system, decision, role, provision, and effective date from current primary sources before making a specific compliance prescription. Neither a general domain label nor this skill establishes a legal duty to use one particular human-review design.

## Evidence discipline for future revisions

Maintain separate evidence for atrophy, formation, misapplication, avoidance, suppression, and pre-emption. A brief framing experiment is not evidence of long-term loss. An incumbent-expert result is not automatically a trainee result. Read later NOVEL INSIGHTS qualifications alongside the original pattern, especially E's challenged production-only premise, Q's termination and capacity conditions, M's task/tool/metric dependence, and T/V's distinction between explanation and detection.

The numerical cases in the companion concept guide remain teaching examples. Do not cite them as real hospitals, employers, trials, or observed recovery programs.
