# Judgment Guard — Mechanisms and design notes

Use the main skill to make the design decision. These notes retain the original mechanisms, worked situations, and practitioner instruments in greater detail. They are hypotheses and design options to assess in context, not guarantees of decline or recovery.

## The original metaphors, clarified

**The cost paid twice** described two possible times to incur a cost: maintaining a needed capability now, or responding to its loss later. It should not imply that the same cost must be paid twice, that prevention is always cheaper, or that every automated task loses valuable expertise. Compare maintenance, recovery, transition, and opportunity costs.

**The three clocks** distinguish an individual's capability, an organization's future supply of expertise, and fidelity along a handoff chain. The relay “clock” is a process dimension, not elapsed months. Their rates require evidence for the actual setting.

**Skill-keeping budget** means explicitly funding practice, mentoring, assessment, or other work that sustains a capability the organization still needs. It is a deliberate capability investment; it should have an objective and evaluation rather than be protected from scrutiny as inherently worthwhile.

**AI gravity** names proposed pressures toward offloading: conserving effort, matching others' performance, and incentives that reward visible output while making AI use difficult to observe. These pressures may be real in a setting. They do not establish an inevitable competitive equilibrium or mean that every individual restraint is futile. Change task design, incentives, coordination, and learning conditions where the diagnosis supports it.

## Drain 0 — Thinkslop

The original four sub-mechanisms are losing intent, outsourcing the thinking that defines the task, failing to preserve useful reasoning, and mistaking agreeable AI feedback for scrutiny. Some occur before an answer; sycophantic reinforcement can also happen during an interaction. The strict claim that all four precede any AI output was too narrow.

Re-establish the decision and useful criteria, then use the AI as a source of alternatives or critique. It may also be a useful collaborator; “foil” and “co-author” are not mutually exclusive roles. Ask for tests and contrary evidence instead of treating either praise or contrarian language as validation. Open-ended exploration can legitimately discover its purpose as it proceeds.

## Drains 1 and 2 — Atrophy, non-formation, and the learning pipeline

The original concern is that reducing practice can weaken existing capability or remove a pathway for newcomers. The mechanism requires a still-needed skill, reduced relevant learning or use, and evidence of an effect. An adoption timeline or a rising AI score alone does not establish it.

Separate **capability debt**, where needed people or learning structures are removed without replacement, from **judgment debt**, where a needed evaluation ability is insufficiently maintained. These are planning metaphors, not financial-accounting categories or measured liabilities by default.

The named-senior-with-junior release practice can combine accountability and apprenticeship when both genuinely inspect or produce relevant work, discuss reasons, and receive useful feedback. A signature with a junior merely present establishes neither. Keep the senior's role meaningful without requiring every expert to personally reproduce every artifact.

Several local sources challenge a production-only theory:

- **Process knowledge and exercise:** Dan Wang's practitioner account emphasizes observation and repeated practice. It does not establish that narration or workshops can never teach, or that all retention requires hands-on production.
- **Interrogating another person's work:** the senior-banker account suggests that active examination of juniors' work may sustain some expertise. It is a reason to distinguish interrogation from passive receipt, not a general proof that production is unnecessary.
- **Mentoring artifacts:** a mentee's draft, prediction, or written account of what the mentor is watching for gives both parties something specific to inspect. Observation and explanation can still contribute; evaluate what the learner can subsequently do.
- **School of Rock's Method App:** the supplied account describes new instructors finding useful scaffolding and some experienced instructors responding better when invited to help shape the library. This supports checking prior task knowledge and perceived agency, not assuming tenure determines the response or that co-authorship always beats incentive changes.
- **A clinical scribe:** the Mass General Brigham account suggests different effects for an attending who no longer drafts a note and a resident who may hear more reasoning. Not recognizing the wording of an AI draft is not proof of clinical atrophy; a possible increase in observable reasoning is not a measured learning gain. Test each role's capability separately.

Do not rely on the original comparison that a radiology miss is always caught the same day while a software error surfaces much later. Detection delays vary in both fields. The useful question is whether relevant feedback reaches the learner soon and accurately enough to support improvement.

## Drain 3 — Handoff degradation

A hiring chain may transform a role need into a job description, a screening rubric, an interview, and a selection recommendation. Each transformation can lose a constraint or add an unsupported assumption. It can also improve the work. Check the interfaces, rather than assume that four stages multiply all their errors.

Attach provenance to consequential claims and preserve access to the original material. Re-anchoring to source can reduce summary-of-summary errors but cannot guarantee accuracy if the source is weak, retrieval selects poorly, or the reader misinterprets it. Data permissions and access must travel with the content.

## Drain 4 — Offloaded ownership

The original three **M** questions are useful:

- **Mindset:** does the person understand how their judgment affects the outcome?
- **Meaning:** do they understand why the review is worth doing?
- **Mechanisms:** do workflow, incentives, and authority support careful evaluation?

An employee-like name or role can influence expectations, but calling a system “Kevin” does not inevitably destroy responsibility. Inspect the whole interaction. A person describing their role as a rubber stamp may be reporting a real authority gap, a workload problem, or an unhelpful interface—not necessarily a lack of motivation.

Recognition and incentives should reward useful detection and sound acceptance. The goal is not to maximize objections. Where automation intentionally owns a low-consequence operation, a constant human approval task may be unnecessary; organizational accountability and required operating controls still need an owner.

## Drain 5 — Motivated avoidance

Keep the distinction between **not feeling responsible** and **avoiding unwelcome information despite responsibility**. The response may require different incentives, less costly access to evidence, or a meaningful review step. A mandatory click may document exposure while adding no understanding.

The Chan research makes explanation uptake a separate question from recommendation uptake and subsequent action. Review the actual experimental context before transferring its effects. Avoiding an explanation may also reflect poor usefulness, ambiguity, workload, or inadequate skill. Do not diagnose intent from one unopened panel.

A relevant disclosure must not be withheld merely because an experiment suggests it might discourage inquiry. Instead, meet the applicable obligation and test how to support useful engagement. Correctly identifying a discriminatory feature and changing a profit-maximizing recommendation are not the same metric as “catching a model error”; define the intended outcome explicitly.

## Drain 6 — Suppression and blocked influence

The professional-services account describes executives expressing certain positions privately but not sustaining them in collective decisions. This raises questions about incentives, coordination, voice, and decision rights. It does not prove that every private position was correct, that no learning occurred, or that every public record is biased toward one answer.

An anonymous before/after question can reveal differences worth investigating. Check whether the wording, audience, new evidence, or research setting changed the response. It is not interchangeable with a seeded-error test, which has a different reference standard.

One proposed response is to give a knowledgeable person an explicit remit to raise a neglected concern and an effective response path. Place that function where it can work; “one level below the CEO” is a case-specific suggestion, not a universal role prescription. Protect the person's capacity and fair treatment rather than merely making silence punishable.

Distinguish never-voiced concerns from voiced-but-dismissed concerns. The first may need a safer or easier route to speak; the second may need improved adjudication, authority, or escalation. Neither is solved automatically by adding an engagement survey.

## Drain 7 — Pre-emption

In the original coaching vignette, an AI summary of a margin review emphasizes supplier costs. That may be accurate while narrowing attention away from another relevant interpretation. The risk is an unexamined frame arriving first, not necessarily a false statement.

For a group, inspect who has source access, how costly it is to check, and whether independent views were collected. The original “number of readers × cost of reopening source” is a qualitative exposure heuristic, not a measured severity equation. A group can reopen evidence during a meeting, and a summary can help both novices and experts when its limits are visible.

Independent first assessments, criteria before outputs, accessible source material, and a named summary owner address different parts of this mechanism. Ordering is one useful intervention, not the entire fix. Versioning and known-omission notes help readers interpret a shared artifact; they cannot enumerate every unknown omission.

## Misapplication — expertise used in the wrong operation

The local KPMG/UT Austin account describes early-career staff who differed in how they directed and revised AI output. Its proposed lesson is that active effort and relevant background do not automatically improve an assisted result. Write suitable evaluation criteria before seeing the answer, then assess whether intervention improves the outcome.

Do not treat the reported behavior profiles as randomized causal treatments. Other group differences may contribute, and a correlation between unassisted and assisted skill would not by itself disprove the possibility of harmful intervention. Use a local comparison to distinguish performance, process, and learning effects.

## Review conditions, friction, and communities

The four named capabilities—uncertainty judgment, systems thinking, ethical escalation, and interpretation through strategy—can be affected by work design. Also inspect whether rules have become difficult to inspect or contest and whether people still have useful relationships for learning. A community can help carry feedback that an individual review form misses.

The three-gear model distinguishes low activation, sustained focus, and high-pressure narrow attention. It is a useful discussion metaphor, not a physiological diagnosis. Its source's air-traffic-control simulation suggests a lightweight interaction might improve engagement in a particular supervisory task; the full study reference and transfer to AI review were not established in the original skill. Test any added interaction for detection, workload, distraction, and gaming before relying on it.

A capable reviewer, inspectable evidence, practical influence, and a termination rule can make a consequential review useful. Paying a cost is neither necessary nor sufficient for independent evidence. AI-generated critique can identify a previously unanticipated problem; its limit is that it is generated analysis, not proof from a separate real stakeholder or empirical test.

A protected advisory setting without performance scoring may encourage exploration. A named decision owner and pre-agreed criteria may prevent endless rework. These serve different purposes: an advisory discussion may legitimately produce questions, while a release review must reach a disposition. Do not force every conversation to close a decision it was not convened to make.

Preserve formative work selectively. Search, evaluation, and interpretation can develop different skills but also interact. Automating exhaustive search may be helpful; do not assert that manually examining data is never important to learning evaluation. Check whether a changed task still offers the experiences the capability needs.

## Repair and responsible records

The elite-coach analogy encourages people to distinguish a poor result from a poor decision and restore a working relationship after a miss. Apply it after urgent harm is contained and without replacing factual investigation. Ask what happened and why; the questions are complementary.

The rule-breaking typology is a prompt to investigate motives and pressures. Its four labels should not become quick judgments about employees. Record relevant evidence, comply with current requirements, and distinguish fair accountability from retaliation for raising a concern. An automatic hard-line response to every alleged legal issue can be as misleading as excusing every well-intended deviation.

The main skill's rationale record captures decision-relevant reasons and conditions for reconsideration. It should support review and learning, not demand private internal thought or equate lengthy prose with expertise. A shorter, clearer rationale can reflect improvement.
